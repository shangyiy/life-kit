# way-outs

The investing use case for risks that lean on a fallback (`/way-outs`).
`SKILL.md` holds the two checks and defers every other principle to
[`investing`](../../core/investing/SKILL.md).

## Credit

These are practitioner accounts, not studies. The first bears directly on
check 1 and the last on check 2; the other two show why being able to leave
matters.

- "Startup比大廠的人生巔峰還香？", conversation with 柯西, 课代表立正, YouTube,
  2021: a risky move judged safe because the way back was thought through.
- "打工人如何獲得財富自由？ | Multiple-FIRE 系列", conversation with Leon,
  课代表立正, YouTube, 2021: being able to walk away is what gives
  bargaining power.
- "Influence Without Authority | 人生元能力", conversation with Leon, 课代表立正,
  YouTube, 2021: a counterpart who knows you cannot leave holds the leverage.
- "如何擺脫無意義的工作？ | Multiple-FIRE 系列", conversation with Leon,
  课代表立正, YouTube, 2023: an option loses value as time passes.

The speakers were successful, so their accounts carry survivorship bias.

## This skill's own framing

The direct source for check 1 is 柯西's "the way back is thought through".
The rest is this skill's framing, not the speakers':

- The three-part test: who catches you, under what conditions, and whether
  the path will still exist.
- That a named catcher without terms still fails, and that the risk that
  looks small only because of a claim is the one to doubt most.
- That a real way out keeps the worst case survivable while the upside stays
  open.
- Check 2: the source's decaying option is a personal timing choice, one that
  loses value with age. Generalising it to any option, "no hurry" when the
  value holds, and "decide, which includes declining" are inferences. The
  source goes further: since you have the option, use it.
