# /way-outs

The use case for taking a risk because you could fall back. Every principle of
[`investing`](../../core/investing/SKILL.md) applies unchanged, above all 1
(avoid permanent loss), 2 (circle of competence) and 5 (opportunity cost, bet
only on clear odds); this skill adds two checks and nothing else. Use it to
make the decision or to review one; in a review, name each breach once, with
its fix.

Inference, not from the sources: a real way out is what keeps the worst case
survivable while the upside stays open. The sources give the two checks below,
not this framing.

## Checks

1. **Is the way out real?** A way out is a path that catches you if the risk
   fails. It counts only if you can say three things: who or what catches you,
   under what conditions, and whether that path will still exist when you need
   it. "It would be fine, I could always go back" is a claim, not a way out,
   until a specific catcher is named. The risk that looks small only because of
   such a claim is the one to doubt most. If no one can be named, decide as if
   there were no way out (`investing` 1). Others can often tell: a counterpart
   who knows you cannot walk away treats your option to leave as empty.
2. **Is the option decaying?** An option is a choice you hold now, and some
   options lose value with time. Ask how much it will be worth if you use it
   later. If it loses value as you wait, waiting has a cost (`investing` 5); if
   it keeps its value, there is no hurry. A decaying option is a reason to
   decide now, never a reason to skip check 1.
