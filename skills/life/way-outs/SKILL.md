# /way-outs

The use case for taking a risk because you could fall back. Every principle of
[`investing`](../../core/investing/SKILL.md) applies unchanged; this skill adds
two checks and nothing else, and cites principles 1 and 5 where they apply. Use
it to make the decision or to review one; in a review, name each breach once,
with its fix.

## Checks

1. **Is the way out real?** A way out is a path that catches you if the risk
   fails. It counts only if you can say who or what catches you, under what
   conditions, and whether that path will still exist when you need it.

   A named person or body without stated terms, or one that may not exist by
   then, still fails. An unstated fallback is a claim, not a way out, and the
   risk that looks small only because of such a claim is the one to doubt
   most. If nothing passes, decide as if there were no way out
   (`investing` 1).
2. **Is the option decaying?** An option is a choice you hold now: the way
   out itself or the chance it backs. Ask whether its value shrinks the longer
   you wait, and how much it will be worth if you use it later. If it
   shrinks, waiting has a cost (`investing` 5); if it keeps its value, there is
   no hurry. Decay is a reason to decide, and declining is a decision; it is
   never a reason to skip check 1.

Inference, not from the sources: a real way out is what keeps the worst case
survivable while the upside stays open. The sources give the two checks in
outline; the README lists what else here is this skill's own framing.
