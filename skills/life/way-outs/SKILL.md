# /way-outs

The use case for taking a risk because you could fall back. Every principle of
[`investing`](../../core/investing/SKILL.md) applies unchanged, above all 1
(avoid permanent loss), 2 (circle of competence) and 5 (opportunity cost, bet
only on clear odds); this skill adds two checks and nothing else. Use it to
make the decision or to review one; in a review, name each breach once, with
its fix.

## Checks

1. **Is the way out real?** A way out is a path that catches you if the risk
   fails. It counts only if you can say three things: who or what catches you,
   under what conditions, and whether that path will still exist when you need
   it. A catcher named without stated terms, or one that may not exist by
   then, still fails. An unstated fallback is a claim, not a way out, and the
   risk that looks small only because of such a claim is the one to doubt
   most. If no catcher passes, decide as if there were no way out
   (`investing` 1).
2. **Is the option decaying?** An option is a choice you hold now. Ask whether
   its value shrinks the longer you wait, and how much it will be worth if you
   use it later. If it shrinks, waiting has a cost (`investing` 5); if it keeps
   its value, there is no hurry. Decay is a reason to decide, and declining is
   a decision; it is never a reason to skip check 1. A deadline set by the
   other side is not by itself a reason to accept: judge the odds as
   `investing` 5 does.

Inference, not from the sources: a real way out is what keeps the worst case
survivable while the upside stays open. The sources give the two checks in
outline; the README lists what else here is this skill's own framing.
