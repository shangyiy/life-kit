# /investment-positioning

Before changing a position, write down why, what would prove it wrong, and
what could force a sale; act only if the case holds. A written case keeps
price swings and fear from making the decision. Apply
[investing](../../core/investing/SKILL.md) throughout.

## Check scope first

Out of scope (see "Stay inside your circle of competence" in investing): a
position size or allocation in numbers, tax or legal advice, retirement-income
planning, debt restructuring, or products whose workings or costs are hidden.
Qualitative calls such as "trim part of it" are fine; never give a number.

Use the Stop output when the whole request is out of scope, when fraud is
suspected (guaranteed returns, pressure to send money), when money has
already been sent, or when the person may be coerced or unable to understand
that losses are possible. When a request mixes in-scope and out-of-scope
parts, answer the in-scope part normally and refer each out-of-scope part in
one line. Priority: Stop over Short over Full.

Never place orders, move money, or request account access. Ask only yes, no,
or don't-know questions, plus the country when a referral needs it; never ask
for amounts, balances, account details, or identifiers.

## Workflow

If the person is panicking or rushed, use the Short output.

1. **Name the decision.** State the change and why it must be decided now.
   Ask: Is this meant to be held past the next review?
2. **State the case.** Say what must be true, what the current price already
   assumes, and what would prove the case wrong. Others agreeing is often
   already in the price, so it is not a reason to buy (see "Think
   independently of the crowd" in investing).
3. **Compare.** Weigh acting against doing nothing and the next-best use of
   the money, including costs (see "Allocate by opportunity cost" and "Cut
   costs, frictions, and taxes"). Value keeping room to act later.
4. **Check for a forced sale.** A forced sale is selling at someone else's
   time or price (see "Avoid big mistakes first"). Run the checklist below.
5. **Record.** Choose buy, add, hold, trim, exit, or wait. Give the strongest
   reason, the strongest objection, what would prove the choice wrong, and
   the observable event that triggers the next review.

## Checklist

- Is margin, borrowed money, or other leverage involved, and could a margin
  call come?
- Is much of the money in this one holding or in closely related ones?
- Could the money be needed, or income stop, before the next review?
- Could a sudden price jump, buyers disappearing, or a lender changing loan
  terms push a sale?
- Would fear of a further fall make the person sell?

## Unknowns

Never treat missing information as safety; use these conservative defaults:

- Case, or what would prove it wrong, unknown: add nothing until both are
  written down.
- Price assumptions or value unknown: claim no edge; gather evidence first.
- Any checklist item unknown or answered don't-know: treat that forced-sale
  risk as present and add nothing. "Add nothing" includes new buys.
- Trim or exit with an unknown case: no irreversible full exit unless a
  forced-sale risk is present; reducing leverage first is fine.
- Missing or conflicting evidence: mark it unknown, say what would resolve
  it, and prefer waiting to invented precision.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a recent gain or loss as the case.
- Averaging down only to improve the recorded purchase price.
- Inventing data, certainty, liquidity, or a precise value.
- Acting to relieve fear, regret, boredom, or urgency.

## Output

- **Stop**: if money was already sent, first say to contact the bank and
  police at once. Then say why it is out of scope, which licensed professional
  to see, and to ask how they are paid (see "Follow the incentives, including
  your advisers'"). For suspected fraud: send no money; check the provider
  with the financial regulator. If coerced, point to someone they trust.
- **Short**: `Pause` (take no irreversible step yet; cutting leverage during
  a margin call is the safe exception), `What is known`, `What is unknown`,
  `Forced-sale checklist` (one line each, leverage first), `Safest next
  action`, and `Review trigger`.
- **Full**: the decision, the case and what the price assumes, the
  comparison, the checklist results, unknowns with their defaults, the
  record from step 5, and one-line referrals for any out-of-scope parts.
