# /investment-positioning

Before changing a position, write down why, what would prove it wrong, and
what could force a sale; act only if the case holds. A written case keeps
price swings and fear from making the decision. Apply
[investing](../../core/investing/SKILL.md) throughout.

## Check scope first

Before anything else, use the Stop output when the request needs an
individualized allocation or position size, tax or legal advice,
retirement-income planning, debt restructuring, or a judgment on an opaque
product. Also stop when someone promises guaranteed returns, pressures the
person to send money, or the person may be coerced or unable to understand
that losses are possible.

Never place orders, move money, or request account access. Ask only yes, no,
or don't-know questions; never ask for amounts, balances, account details,
or identifiers, and use only what is volunteered.

## Workflow

If the person is panicking or rushed, skip to the Short output: it keeps
only step 4's checklist and takes no irreversible step yet.

1. **Name the decision.** State the change and why it must be decided now.
   Ask: Is this meant to be held past the next review?
2. **State the case.** Say what must be true, what the current price already
   assumes, and what would prove the case wrong. Others agreeing is already
   in the price, so it is not a reason to buy (see "Think independently of
   the crowd" in investing).
3. **Compare.** Weigh acting against doing nothing and the next-best use of
   the money, including costs (see "Allocate by opportunity cost" and "Cut
   costs, frictions, and taxes"). Value keeping room to act later.
4. **Check for a forced sale.** A forced sale is selling at someone else's
   time or price. Run the checklist below.
5. **Record.** Choose buy, add, hold, trim, exit, or wait. Give the strongest
   reason, the strongest objection, what would prove the choice wrong, and
   the observable event that triggers the next review.

## Checklist

- Is margin, borrowed money, or other leverage involved, and could a margin
  call come?
- Could the money be needed before the next review?
- Could income stop?
- Could a sudden price jump, vanished liquidity, or changed financing terms
  push a sale?
- Would fear of a further loss make the person sell?

## Unknowns

Never treat missing information as safety. Turn each material unknown into a
conservative default:

- Case, or what would prove it wrong, unknown: add nothing until both are
  written down.
- Price assumptions or valuation unknown: claim no edge; gather current
  evidence first.
- Leverage, financing, cash needs, income, or reaction to loss unknown, or
  answered don't-know: add nothing.
- For trim or exit with an unknown case: avoid an irreversible full exit
  unless a forced-sale risk is present; reducing leverage first is fine.
- Missing or conflicting evidence: mark it unknown, say what would resolve
  it, and prefer waiting to invented precision.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a recent gain or loss as the case.
- Averaging down only to improve the recorded purchase price.
- Inventing data, certainty, liquidity, or a precise value.
- Acting to relieve fear, regret, boredom, or urgency.

## Output

Return exactly one:

- **Stop**: why the request is outside this skill and what kind of licensed
  professional to see; remind the person to ask how that professional is
  paid (see "Follow the incentives, including your advisers'"). If coerced,
  also point to someone they trust. For suspected fraud: send no money,
  check the provider with the financial regulator, and if money was already
  sent, contact the bank at once and report it to the police.
- **Short**: `Pause`, `What is known`, `What is unknown`, `Forced-sale
  checklist` (one line each, leverage and margin calls first), `Safest next
  action`, and `Review trigger`.
- **Full**: the decision, the case and what the price assumes, the
  comparison, the checklist results, unknowns with their defaults, and the
  record from step 5.
