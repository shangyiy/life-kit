# /investment-positioning

Before changing a position, write down why, what would prove it wrong, and what
could force a sale; act only if the case holds. A written case keeps price
swings and fear from making the decision. Apply
[investing](../../core/investing/SKILL.md) throughout.

## Workflow

1. **Check scope.** Out of scope (see "Stay inside your circle of competence"):
   any position size or allocation in numbers, tax or legal advice,
   retirement-income planning, debt restructuring, or products with hidden
   workings or costs. Qualitative calls without numbers are fine. Use Stop when
   the whole request is out of scope, when the person is being defrauded or has
   sent money to a scheme, or they may be coerced or unable to understand that
   losses are possible. A held company accused of fraud is not a Stop case; use
   Short or Full. For a mixed request, answer the in-scope part and refer each
   out-of-scope part in one line. Use Short when the person is panicking or
   rushed, otherwise Full; Stop wins over Short, Short over Full.
2. **Name the decision.** State the change and why it must be decided now.
   For a buy or hold, ask: Is this meant to be held past the next review? For
   a sale, ask: Is anything forcing this sale?
3. **State the case.** The case is the person's reason for this change; if they
   give none, record that. Say what must be true, what the current price
   already assumes, and what would prove the case wrong. Others agreeing is
   often already in the price, so it is not a reason to buy (see "Think
   independently of the crowd" in investing).
4. **Compare.** Weigh acting against doing nothing and the next-best use of the
   money, including costs and taxes, without giving tax advice (see "Allocate
   by opportunity cost" and "Cut costs, frictions, and taxes"). Value keeping
   room to act later.
5. **Check for a forced sale.** A forced sale is selling at someone else's
   time or price (see "Avoid big mistakes first"). Run the checklist below.
6. **Record.** Choose buy, add, hold, trim, exit, or wait. Give the strongest
   reason, the strongest objection, what would prove the choice wrong, and
   the observable event that triggers the next review.

Never place orders, move money, or request account access. Ask about the
person's situation only with yes, no, or don't-know questions. The one open
question allowed is which country they are in, and only when a referral needs
it. Never ask for amounts, balances, account details, or identifiers.

## Checklist

What could force a sale:

- Is borrowed money involved, and could a lender demand more cash or force a
  sale (a margin call)?
- Is much of the money in this one holding or in closely related ones?
- Could the money be needed, or income stop, before the next review?
- Could a sudden price jump, buyers disappearing, or a lender changing loan
  terms push a sale?
- Would fear of a further fall make the person sell?

Defaults when something is unknown (missing information is never safety):

- Case, or what would prove it wrong, unknown: add nothing until both are
  written down.
- Price assumptions or value unknown: claim no advantage; gather evidence first.
- A forced-sale risk unknown or answered don't-know: treat it as present and
  add nothing, not even new buys.
- Under that same unknown, while the case holds, no full exit unless the
  person confirms the risk; trimming and repaying investment loans are fine.
- Missing or conflicting evidence: mark it unknown, say what would resolve
  it, and prefer waiting to invented precision.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a recent gain or loss as the case.
- Buying more after a fall only to lower the average purchase price.
- Inventing data, certainty, willing buyers, or a precise value.
- Acting to relieve fear, regret, boredom, or urgency.

## Output

- **Stop**: if the person is being defrauded or has sent money, say to contact
  the bank at once to try to recall the transfer, keep all evidence, report to
  the police and the financial regulator, send no more money, and beware anyone
  offering to recover lost money. If coerced, point to someone they trust.
  Otherwise, say why it is out of scope, which licensed professional to see,
  and to ask how they are paid (see "Follow the incentives").
- **Short**: runs steps 1, 2, and 5 only. Sections: `What to hold off on` (any
  irreversible step), `What is known`, `What is unknown`, `What could force a
  sale` (one line each, borrowing first), `Safest next action`, `Review
  trigger`, and `Referrals` (one line each, if any). In a margin call, check
  the deadline; meet it with spare cash or a partial sale, never a new loan.
- **Full**: the decision, the case and what the price assumes, the
  comparison, the checklist results, unknown defaults applied, the record
  from step 6, and one-line referrals for any out-of-scope parts.
