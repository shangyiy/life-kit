# /investment-positioning

Before changing a position, write down why, what would prove it wrong, and
whether anything could force you to sell, then act only if the case still
holds. A written case keeps price swings and fear from making the decision.
Apply [investing](../../core/investing/SKILL.md) throughout.

## Check scope first

Before deciding anything, use the Stop format when the request needs an
individualized allocation or position size, tax or legal advice,
retirement-income planning, debt restructuring, or a judgment on an opaque
product. Also stop when someone promises guaranteed returns, pressures the
person to send money, or the person may be coerced or unable to understand
that losses are possible.

Never place orders, move money, or request account access. Ask only yes, no,
or don't-know questions that need no amounts; never ask for balances,
account details, or identifiers, and use only what is volunteered.

## Decide

1. **Name the decision.** State the change, the holding period, and why it
   must be decided now. If the person is panicking or rushed, use the Short
   format and take no irreversible step yet.
2. **State the case.** Say what must be true, what the current price already
   assumes, and what would prove the case wrong. Others agreeing is already
   in the price; it is not a reason to buy (core principle 9).
3. **Compare.** Weigh acting against doing nothing and the next-best use of
   the money, including costs (core principles 5 and 7). Value keeping room
   to act later.
4. **Check for a forced sale.** A forced sale is selling at someone else's
   time or price. Ask: Is margin, borrowed money, or other leverage involved?
   Could the money be needed before the next review? Could income stop? Would
   a loss larger than expected, a sudden price jump, vanished liquidity, or
   changed financing terms push a sale? Would fear make the person sell?
5. **Record.** Choose buy, add, hold, trim, exit, or wait. Give the strongest
   reason, the strongest objection, what would prove the choice wrong, and
   the observable event that triggers the next review.

## Unknowns

Never treat missing information as safety. Turn each material unknown into a
conservative action:

- Case, or what would prove it wrong, unknown: add nothing until both are
  written down.
- Price assumptions or valuation unknown: claim no edge; gather current
  evidence first.
- Leverage, financing, or true exposure unknown: add nothing until the
  obligation and worst-case loss are known.
- A don't-know on cash needs, income, or reaction to loss: add nothing.
- Missing or conflicting evidence: mark it unknown, say what would resolve
  it, and prefer waiting to invented precision.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a recent gain or loss as the case.
- Averaging down only to improve the recorded purchase price.
- Inventing data, certainty, liquidity, or a precise value.
- Acting to relieve fear, regret, boredom, or urgency.

## Formats

Return exactly one format, then end with: "Educational only; this is not
individualized financial advice."

- **Stop**: why the request is outside this skill and what kind of licensed
  professional to see, reminding the person to ask how that professional is
  paid (core principle 4). For suspected fraud, add: send no money, and check
  the provider with the financial regulator.
- **Short**, when the person is panicking or rushed: `Pause`, `What is known`,
  `What is unknown`, `Safest next action`, and `Review trigger`.
- **Full**, otherwise: the decision, the case and what the price assumes, the
  comparison, the forced-sale result, unknowns with their actions, and the
  record from step 5.
