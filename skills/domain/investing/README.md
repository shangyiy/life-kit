# investing

Principles for deciding where to put capital, what to pay, and when to act or
wait (`/investing`). `SKILL.md` holds the principles, most important first.
The order is the editor's judgment of emphasis in the source; the source's own
checklist says its principles cannot "necessarily be prioritized".

## Credit

Each principle restates Charles T. Munger, *Poor Charlie's Almanack: The
Essential Wit and Wisdom of Charles T. Munger*, ed. Peter D. Kaufman (Stripe
Press, 2023; first published 2005), read in the free online edition
([text](https://www.stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/book)). That edition
has no page numbers, so sources are named by chapter or talk.

1. **Avoid big mistakes; margin of safety; leverage**: Chapter Three
   (investing checklist: "All investment evaluations should begin by
   measuring risk, especially reputational."; "Avoid big mistakes; shun
   permanent capital loss."); Talk One and Talk Three (inversion); Talk Six
   (Long-Term Capital Management and leverage).
2. **Circle of competence**: Chapter Three ("yes, no, and too tough to
   understand"); Talk Two; Talk Three ("you’ve got to know what you know and
   what you don’t know.").
3. **Do not fool yourself; misjudgment checklist**: Talk Six (quoting
   Feynman); Talk One (Darwin and disconfirming evidence); Talk Ten (stating
   the opposing case); Talk Three and Talk Eleven (the standard causes of
   misjudgment and their combined, "lollapalooza", effects).
4. **Incentives and advisers**: Talk Two; Talk Four; Talk Eleven ("Especially
   fear professional advice when it is especially good for the advisor");
   Talk Three (cases where "your best course is to trust some expert").
5. **Opportunity cost; bet heavily only on clear odds; temperament**: Chapter
   Three (allocation checklist); Talk Two ("They bet big when they have the
   odds. And the rest of the time, they don’t."); Talk Three (recognizable
   odds; "a very conservative pattern of investment" if losses would make you
   miserable).
6. **Business, then price**: Talk Two ("it’s hard for a stock to earn a much
   better return than the business that underlies it earns"; too-high prices);
   Chapter Three (great business at a fair price).
7. **Costs, frictions, taxes, indexing**: Chapter Three ("never take action
   for its own sake"); Talk Two (taxes); Talk Six (croupiers' costs; indexing
   "a wiser choice for the average foundation").
8. **Big ideas as a checklist; forward and reverse**: Talk Two; Talk Four;
   Talk Five; Talk Nine ("use those tools checklist-style"); Talk Ten.
9. **Independence from the crowd**: Chapter Three ("Mimicking the herd invites
   regression to the mean"); Talk Eleven (social proof); Talk Three (market
   agnosticism).
10. **Fix mistakes; keep learning**: Talk Three (quitting "a much-loved
    hand"); Talk Eleven (deprival-superreaction); Talk Ten (wisdom as a duty;
    anticipating trouble).
