# /grouping

The domain of grouping: putting observations into kinds that can be named,
counted and acted on, whether the kinds are found or given in advance. Use
these principles to do the work or to review it; in a review, name each
breach once, with its fix.

## Principles

1. **The purpose fixes the relation.** State the question the groups answer
   and the relation that puts two observations together. Group by it, not by
   surface likeness such as shared form, or by having occurred together.
2. **Found kinds come from the observations.** Unless the kinds are given,
   build them from the observations. Familiar categories pull what you see
   toward them: they may help list observations but never form the groups.
   Hold each kind as provisional until it earns its place.
3. **Dense inside, distinct between.** Members cohere; any two groups differ
   in a way you can state. Groups at one level are the same kind of thing.
4. **Name each group by what its members share,** specifically enough to
   decide from the name whether an observation belongs; a higher group's name
   sums up those below it. A label that only says a group exists, or borrows
   a familiar category, is not a name. Define each group in a sentence or
   two: what it includes, what it excludes, how it differs from its neighbor.
5. **Account for every observation.** Each is in a group or stays alone with
   its reason. Challenge a catch-all group: it may hold kinds not yet found.
   Keep an exception as a variation or a new kind.
6. **Someone who did not build the groups checks them.** Where they will be
   counted or decide something, that reader assigns a sample by the
   definitions alone; report agreement per group, against a bar fixed in
   advance by the cost of a wrong conclusion. Otherwise the reader checks
   that each group follows from its members. A disagreement is a finding
   about a definition: sharpen it and check again. Agreement shows that
   others can reproduce the groups, not that they are right.
7. **Check against observations that did not make the groups.** Reread the
   whole set against the final groups, including what formed none of them.
8. **Compare each observation with the groups so far.** Group like with
   like, and only like. Split a group that holds two kinds; merge two groups
   that are one. Stop when new observations no longer change them.
9. **Fix the unit before grouping:** what one observation is, at a
   comparable scope and a size the purpose sets, and whether it may sit in
   several groups.
10. **Count only settled kinds, and say what one count is.** Size alone does
    not make a group important; importance follows the purpose.

## Definitions

- **Group (kind):** observations that share the relation the purpose names.
  Given groups are fixed in advance and applied by their definitions.
- **Agreement:** how often independent readers place the same observations
  in the same groups, beyond chance.
