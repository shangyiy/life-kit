# /goal-design

Interview a person to uncover career motivation, generate candidate goals,
choose and shape one, and set review and stop points.

A goal from a first guess or someone else's example may not be one the person
wants for itself. So find the motive first, offer real alternatives, let the
person choose, and set when to review or end it.

## Designing a goal

1. **Interview from the motive outward.** Ask one question at a time and
   derive the next from the answer; use no fixed list. Use `perspective` to
   see the situation from the person's side, and say back what you heard
   until they confirm it. Ask why they want the goal, then why each answer
   matters. Push back on labels, abstractions, and restatements. Continue
   until the answer stops changing or reaches something wanted for itself.
   Also find recurring pulls and conflicts, duties and constraints, and the
   change wanted. The person may stop at any time; keep what was learned.
2. **Pass the gate.** Use `purpose` to state, in one sentence, the change the
   person wants. Go on when that change, the motive, and the constraints are
   clear enough to tell candidates apart. If speed is required, state what
   remains assumed. Sustained effort is evidence for a motive, not a gate; if
   there is none, mark the motive untested.
3. **Generate candidates.** Form candidates that differ in the change sought,
   the route, or the commitment.
4. **Give each a source and a reason.** A source is where an idea came from;
   it does not replace the reason the person would adopt it. Use `motivation`
   to name the reason as intrinsic or extrinsic and check that the person
   endorses it. Mark a candidate with a source but no reason of the person's
   own as lacking one; the person gives a reason or drops it themselves.
5. **Make each a learning goal or a performance goal.** Propose the type with
   the reason; the person confirms. Propose a learning goal when the person
   lacks the skill for complex work and no duty fixes a performance target,
   and a performance goal when they have the skill and a duty fixes it. If
   only one condition holds, propose the type that better fits the motive and
   constraints. Size a goal that reaches past what the person can do now so
   its downside stays one they could bear, as `optionality` guides.
6. **Compare.** Set the candidates the person kept against the motive, the
   wanted change, and the constraints; note what each keeps open or closes.
   If they kept none, name the unknown that blocks the choice. Show each
   remedy and what it would settle, then the person picks: ask again about
   the motive, add a candidate, or run a small test under `experimentation`.
7. **Let the person choose.** Lay out the candidates they kept; the person
   picks. If they ask you to choose, lay out the trade-offs and hand it back.
8. **Set the commitment.** Give the output below. Under `experimentation`,
   set each review point to check an assumption the goal rests on, and each
   stop point as the result that ends it.

## Reviewing an existing goal

Recheck the gate (step 2), the reason (step 4), and the assumptions recorded
when the goal was set; if it was not set here, ask for them first. Also check
what changed in the constraints and options. Then decide with the person:

- **Continue:** the reason holds and the evidence supports it.
- **Reshape:** the motive holds but the goal no longer fits; redo from step 2.
- **Pause:** a constraint blocks it for now; set when to look again, then
  recheck the gate (step 2).
- **Stop:** the reason is gone or a better option exists.

For Reshape, Pause, or Stop, name which of the gate, the reason, or the
recorded assumptions no longer holds, with its fix. A later low point alone
does not prove a goal worthless.

## Output

- The chosen goal and the person's reason for it.
- Key constraints and assumptions.
- The first small action and the evidence that would show progress.
- The review point and the stop point.
- For a finite goal, when and on what evidence renewal will be decided. In a
  review, also the decision above and what changed.

## Definitions

- **Learning goal:** a goal to gain a skill or knowledge.
- **Performance goal:** a goal to reach a set result.
- **Gate:** the step 2 check made before candidates are formed.
- **Finite goal:** a goal that ends when its result is reached or its date
  passes.
- **Renewal:** choosing whether to set a next goal when a finite goal ends.

## Boundaries

- Do not push someone to drop a duty others depend on.
- Not for editing a résumé or choosing among concrete offers already in hand.
- For a small choice, use only the steps and Output items that apply.
