# /goal-design

Interview a person to uncover career motivation, generate candidate goals,
choose and shape one, and set review and stop points.

A goal taken from a first guess or from someone else's example may not be one
the person wants for itself. So find the motive first, offer real
alternatives, let the person choose, and set when the goal is reviewed or
ended.

## Designing a goal

1. **Interview from the motive outward.** Ask one question at a time and
   derive the next from the answer; use no fixed list. Use `perspective` to
   see the situation from the person's side, and say back what you heard
   until they confirm it. Ask why they want the goal, then why each answer
   matters. Push back on labels, abstractions, and restatements. Continue
   until the answer stops changing or reaches something wanted for itself;
   either person may stop. Also find recurring pulls and conflicts, duties and
   constraints, and the change wanted.
2. **Pass the gate.** Use `purpose` to state, in one sentence, the change the
   person wants. Go on when that change, the motive, and the constraints are
   clear enough to tell candidates apart. If speed is required, state what
   remains assumed. Sustained effort is evidence for a motive, not a gate; if
   there is none, mark the motive untested.
3. **Generate candidates.** Form candidates that differ in the change sought,
   the route, or the commitment.
4. **Give each a source and a reason.** A source only says where an idea came
   from, such as a comparison, a role model, or what others expect; it does
   not replace the reason for adopting it. Use `motivation` to name the reason
   as intrinsic or extrinsic and check that the person endorses it. Drop a
   candidate that has a source but no reason of the person's own.
5. **Make each a learning goal or a performance goal.** When the person lacks
   the skill for complex work and no duty fixes a performance target, prefer a
   learning goal. Set any stretch under `optionality`.
6. **Compare.** Set the candidates against the motive, the wanted change, and
   the constraints, and note what each keeps open or closes. If no candidate
   is left, name the unknown that blocks the choice and probe it: ask again
   about the motive, add a candidate, or run a small test under
   `experimentation`.
7. **Let the person choose.** Lay out the survivors; the person picks.
8. **Set the commitment.** Give the output below, with each review and stop
   point set under `experimentation`.

## Reviewing an existing goal

Recheck the reason the person adopted it, which assumptions now have evidence,
and what has changed in the constraints and options. Then decide with the
person:

- **Continue:** the reason holds and the evidence supports it.
- **Reshape:** the motive holds but the goal no longer fits; return to step 3.
- **Pause:** a constraint blocks it for now; set when to look again.
- **Stop:** the reason is gone or a better option exists.

Name each failed check once, with its fix. For a finite goal, decide renewal
and what follows; a later low alone does not prove it worthless.

## Output

- The chosen goal and the person's reason for it.
- Key constraints and assumptions.
- The first small action and the evidence that would show progress.
- The review point and the stop condition.
- For a finite goal, the renewal decision. In a review, also the decision
  above and what changed.

## Definitions

- **Source:** where a candidate goal came from.
- **Reason:** why the person would adopt that goal.

## Boundaries

- The person chooses; do not choose for them.
- Do not push someone to drop a duty others depend on.
