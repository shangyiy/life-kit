# /goal-design

Interview a person to uncover career motivation, generate candidate goals,
choose and shape one, and set review and stop points.

A goal from a first guess or someone else's example may not be one the person
wants for itself. So find the motive first, offer real alternatives, let the
person choose, and set when to review or end it.

## Designing a goal

1. **Interview from the motive outward.** Ask one question at a time, each
   derived from the last answer. Use `perspective` to see the situation from the
   person's side, and say back what you heard until they confirm it. Ask why
   they want the goal, then why each answer matters. Push back on labels,
   abstractions, and restatements. Continue until the answer stops changing or
   reaches something wanted for itself. Also find what keeps drawing them and
   what conflicts with it, their skills, duties, and constraints, and the change
   wanted. The person may stop at any time; keep what was learned.
2. **Pass the gate.** Use `purpose` to state, in one sentence, the change the
   person wants. Go on when that change, the motive, and the constraints are
   clear enough to tell candidates apart; if not, return to step 1. If the
   person wants to move fast, state what remains assumed. Sustained effort is
   evidence for a motive, not a gate; with none, mark the motive untested.
3. **Generate candidates** that differ in the change, route, or commitment.
4. **Give each a source and a reason.** A source is where an idea came from; it
   does not replace the reason the person would adopt it. Use `motivation` to
   name the reason as intrinsic or extrinsic and check that the person endorses
   it. A candidate with a source but no reason of the person's own is marked as
   lacking one; it stays until the person gives a reason or drops it. Settle
   each before step 6 (the others can go on to step 5). A candidate is kept when
   it has the person's reason.
5. **Make each a learning goal or a performance goal.** Propose the type with
   the reason; the person confirms or changes it. If the person lacks the skill,
   propose a learning goal, followed by the target if a duty fixes one; if they
   have the skill, a performance goal, the default. Also propose a size; if the
   goal reaches past what the person can do now, keep its downside one they
   could bear, as `optionality` guides. The person agrees or changes it.
6. **Compare.** Set the kept candidates against the motive, the wanted change,
   and the constraints; note what each keeps open or closes. If none was kept,
   name what blocks the choice; the person picks a way forward: ask again about
   the motive (step 1), add a candidate (step 3), or run a small test under
   `experimentation` and take its result to step 4.
7. **Let the person choose.** Lay out the kept candidates; the person picks
   (with one, adopts it or returns to step 3). If asked to choose, give the
   trade-offs and hand it back.
8. **Set the commitment.** Propose the Output below; the person agrees or
   changes it. Under `experimentation`, review points test its assumptions.

## Reviewing an existing goal

Recheck the gate (step 2), the reason (step 4), the assumptions recorded when
the goal was set (ask for them if not set here), and any change in constraints
or options. If the motive is unclear or untested, return to step 1 first. Decide
with the person; add the decision and change to the Output:

- **Continue:** the reason holds and the evidence supports it.
- **Reshape:** the motive holds but the goal or its reason no longer fits; redo
  from step 2, keeping earlier candidates the person still has a reason for.
- **Pause:** a constraint blocks it for now; set a date, then recheck the gate.
- **Stop:** the motive is gone, a better option replaces the goal, or the stop
  point is reached; for a finite goal, decide renewal then.

For Reshape and Pause, name which of the wanted change, the constraints, the
reason, or the recorded assumptions no longer holds, and the fix; for Stop, name
what ended it. A later low point alone does not prove a goal worthless.

## Output

- The chosen goal, the person's reason, and key constraints and assumptions.
- The first small action and the evidence that would show progress.
- The review point and the stop point.
- For a finite goal, when and on what evidence renewal will be decided.

## Definitions

- **Learning goal:** a goal to gain a skill or knowledge. **Performance goal:**
  a goal to reach a set result.
- **Gate:** the step 2 check, made first and again at each review.
- **Review point:** a time or event to check that the goal still holds.
- **Stop point:** when the person ends the goal: a result, a date, or for a goal
  with no end, evidence that it no longer serves the motive.
- **Finite goal:** a goal that ends when its result is reached or its date
  passes. **Renewal:** choosing whether to set a next goal then.

## Boundaries

- Do not push someone to drop a duty others depend on.
- Not for editing a résumé or choosing among concrete offers already in hand.
- For a small choice, use only the steps and Output items that apply.
