# goal-design

## Credit

- Step 1's habit of asking why until the answer is wanted for itself
  resembles laddering, and draws on it by analogy: Thomas J. Reynolds and
  Jonathan Gutman, "Laddering Theory, Method, Analysis, and Interpretation,"
  *Journal of Advertising Research* 28(1), 1988, pp. 11-31.
- Choosing between a learning goal and a specific, challenging performance goal
  draws on Edwin A. Locke and Gary P. Latham, "Building a Practically Useful
  Theory of Goal Setting and Task Motivation: A 35-Year Odyssey," *American
  Psychologist* 57(9), 2002, pp. 705-717.
- Deciding on a next goal before one that ends at a result or date runs out
  draws by analogy on Jesper Juul, "The Open and the Closed: Games of Emergence
  and Games of Progression," in Frans Mäyrä, ed., *Computer Games and Digital
  Cultures Conference Proceedings*, Tampere University Press, 2002, pp. 323-329;
  the career use is the author's own.
- The caution that a later low does not prove a goal worthless draws by
  analogy on Karen Howells and Mathijs Lucassen, "'Post-Olympic
  Blues'--The Diminution of Celebrity in Olympic Athletes," *Psychology of
  Sport and Exercise* 37, 2018, pp. 67-78. That qualitative study concerned
  four Olympians, so it supports a possibility, not a universal claim.
- Everything else is the author's own.
