# negotiation

A use case for reaching an agreement that serves the important
interests of the people involved, or seeing that none is better
(`/negotiation`). The same approach fits pay, project scope, shared
resources, personal arrangements and business terms.

## Credit

- Roger Fisher, William Ury and Bruce Patton, *Getting to Yes: Negotiating Agreement Without Giving In*, Penguin Books, 2011, summarized by the Harvard Program on Negotiation, "Principled Negotiation: Focus on Interests to Create Value," https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/negotiation-skills-daily/principled-negotiation-focus-interests-create-value/: interests over positions, options for mutual gain, objective criteria, and the best alternative to a deal (Principles, steps 1-5).
- Haseeb Qureshi, "Ten Rules for Negotiating a Job Offer," 2016, https://haseebq.com/my-ten-rules-for-negotiating-a-job-offer/: keeping a written record, not deciding on the spot, taking time, and protecting information (Principles, steps 5-6).
- Haseeb Qureshi, "How Not to Bomb Your Offer Negotiation," 2016, https://haseebq.com/how-not-to-bomb-your-offer-negotiation/: a reason with each request (the skill requires it to be true), honesty about everything else, trading across terms, and a decision time (Principles, steps 4-6).
- Leon (guest), "Influence Without Authority | 人生元能力," 课代表立正 (YouTube), 2021-07-27, https://youtu.be/CTcMvIZFQcw, 33:23-37:30: interests over positions, a first "no" as a start, and walking away as leverage (Principles, steps 2, 3, 5 and 6); the video has no official captions, so the ideas are restated loosely.
- Everything else is the author's own: the five principles, the order and grouping of the six steps, the honesty limits, the output, and how the four core skills fit.
