# /negotiation

A negotiation succeeds when both sides find terms that serve their important
interests better than what each could do without a deal; if no such terms
exist, no deal is the right result.

Work through the five parts below in order, going back as you learn more.

## Interests

Each side states a position, but what it needs is the interests behind it. A
position is one way to meet them; there are usually others.

Use [`/purpose`](../../core/purpose/SKILL.md) to state the outcome you want.
Sort what you want into essential, important and tradeable, and set a first
minimum. Use [`/perspective`](../../core/perspective/SKILL.md) to learn what
the other side wants, what it answers for, and its limits and incentives.

## Information

What each side knows about limits, priorities, alternatives and who decides
shapes what it will accept. Protecting information is fine; deceiving is not.

Ask before you propose, and list what you still assume. Learn who can decide
and when. Share your interests and the true reason for each request; keep
your minimum and alternatives to yourself unless sharing them helps. Do not
bluff about alternatives or invent someone you must consult.

## Alternatives

A deal is worth making only if it beats what you would do without it.

Use [`/optionality`](../../core/optionality/SKILL.md) to judge what happens
with no deal, your best alternative and how strong it is, and theirs. Revise
your minimum to match. If your alternative is weak, improve it or buy time
before you commit. Then decide whether it is worth negotiating at all: weigh
what is at stake against the cost to the relationship and the risk of asking.

## Value creation

Sides weigh issues, risk and timing differently. Those gaps let each give what
costs it little and gain what matters to it.

Find several ways to meet both sets of interests, trading across issues and
testing them against fair outside standards. A proposal may be a package, or
a different deal, such as smaller scope or a first step that lowers their
risk. Know what each concession costs you and what it gives them, and ask the
reasons behind an objection. Where future results are uncertain, use
[`/experimentation`](../../core/experimentation/SKILL.md) for a limited
pilot, milestones or conditional terms.

## Commitment

An agreement is useful only when both sides understand its terms the same way
and can realistically keep them.

Agree when each side will decide, leaving time to judge the whole before you
accept or refuse. Confirm the terms in writing, with the assumptions left.
Name responsibilities, deliverables, dates and who decides. Set review points
and what reopens the deal. If the terms miss your minimum, decline and turn
to your best alternative.

## Output

- Your interests, minimum and best alternative, and whether negotiating was
  worth it.
- Their interests, limits, who decides, and unchecked assumptions.
- The options weighed and the terms agreed, in writing.
- Responsibilities, deliverables, dates, decision rights and review points.
- The decision: agree, or no deal, and the next message.
