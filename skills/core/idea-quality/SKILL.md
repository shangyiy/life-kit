# Idea quality

Judge an idea by what it says, not by how much attention it gets. These checks hold for any idea in any field; whoever runs this says which ones apply.

## Checks
- **Makes sense from first principles.** You can explain why it should work from the underlying mechanism, not because someone says so or because it looks clever. Where its design decisions are written down, judge their quality the same way: each choice should follow from the problem and weigh what it gives up. Missing write-ups cost nothing; supporting evidence often lives elsewhere.
- **One simple, cohesive idea.** Its core fits in one sentence, and every part serves that sentence. A grab-bag of loosely related tricks fails, however polished.
- **Organic.** The author built it for their own problem and keeps using it (Paul Graham's word for such ideas). No proof required. Skip this check for releases from companies or labs, where it does not apply.

## Example
A method made of four lenses applied across six lanes, each a separate trick: it fails cohesion, since no one sentence holds it together. A little-known guide that teaches one way to write requirements, made by someone who uses it in their own work, and whose choices each follow from that one way: it passes.

## Why
The first check is first-principles reasoning: trust a mechanism you can explain over an authority. The second is cohesion from design (parts that belong together, nothing else). The third comes from Paul Graham's "How to Get Startup Ideas" (2012): the best ideas come from people solving their own problems.
