# /idea-quality

What a good idea looks like, in any field, whatever its popularity or whoever
recommends it. A good idea is like a pattern in a pattern language: someone
keeps meeting the same problem in real use and finds one core solution that
resolves it, and that solution works alongside others.

## Principles

1. **A good idea answers a problem that someone observed recurring in their own
   work.** The source shows who kept running into the problem and that the idea
   grew from that experience. The problem is also common: others keep running
   into it too, and the idea actually helps them. A problem invented to fit a
   solution is not such a problem. A feature copied from another product to
   keep up with it is evidence against the idea, not a gap in what is known.
   When the source shows no such problem, the idea has not yet shown that it is
   good. Popularity alone does not show that the problem is real.
2. **A good idea is one simple, cohesive idea that makes sense from first
   principles.** It fits in one sentence: one problem, one mechanism. Why the
   mechanism works follows from basic facts, not from "it is like X" or "others
   do it". It may have several parts, as long as they all serve that one
   problem for the same reason. A part that serves nothing the reader needs is
   an add-on: name it, but it does not change what the core is worth. Only when
   each part solves its own separate problem and no core ties them together is
   it several ideas.
3. **A good idea removes the problem when it runs, not only describes it
   well.** It need not remove all of the problem: a clear statement of what
   matters, backed by a concrete action that moves the hard part, is enough.
   Ask where the hard part of the problem goes. A solution that only restates
   the goal and trusts the worker to keep to it leaves the hard part untouched,
   and nothing handles the gap between the reading of the goal and the real
   need. The idea's own account of its problem and mechanism is not proof that
   it works.
4. **A good idea composes with other ideas.** It does one job and hands the
   rest to other practices, instead of trying to do everything itself. That
   lets it work alongside them.
