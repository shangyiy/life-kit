# /idea-quality

The domain of judging whether an idea is good. Judge an idea by what it is
and where it came from, not by its popularity or who recommends it. These
principles hold for any idea in any field; whoever runs this says which apply.

## Principles

1. **Useful: it solves a common, recurring problem.** Name the problem and
   who keeps hitting it. If you cannot, the idea is not useful yet, however
   popular it is.
2. **One simple, cohesive idea that makes sense from first principles.** It
   can be stated as one problem and one mechanism in a sentence, and you can
   explain why the mechanism works from what is surely true, not from "it is
   like X" or "others do it". A bundle of unrelated ideas fails; judge each
   idea in it on its own.
3. **Organic: it grew from the author's or the organization's own need.**
   That is evidence the problem is real. When the text cannot show it, leave
   it unknown rather than count it against the idea.

## Definitions

- **Use / value underneath:** what a person does with it, and the lasting
  problem it solves, whatever the tool. What is new is how it attacks that
  problem.
- **What is new:** the usual approach's bottleneck (its cost when known), the
  one move that removes or bypasses it, and whether that is a new way or the
  same way faster. Not its results. The same way faster is labelled faster,
  not new; it matters only when the speed-up changes what people can do.
