# experimentation

Principles for using a small, cheap, reversible test when thinking cannot
settle an uncertainty (`/experimentation`).

## Credit

- Principles 1, 3, and 5 draw on Herminia Ibarra, *Working Identity:
  Unconventional Strategies for Reinventing Your Career*, Harvard Business
  School Press, 2003, for learning through action and trying several possible
  paths before committing.
- Principle 3 also draws on Amy C. Edmondson, “Strategies for Learning from
  Failure,” *Harvard Business Review* 89(4), 2011, for keeping tests at the
  smallest useful scale.
- Principle 3 draws on Saras D. Sarasvathy, “Causation and Effectuation:
  Toward a Theoretical Shift from Economic Inevitability to Entrepreneurial
  Contingency,” *Academy of Management Review* 26(2), 2001, for risking only
  what one can afford to lose.
- Principle 2 draws on Tim Ferriss, “Why you should define your fears instead
  of your goals,” TED talk, 2017-04-24,
  https://www.ted.com/talks/tim_ferriss_why_you_should_define_your_fears_instead_of_your_goals,
  for writing down the worst case, prevention, and repair.
- Principle 1 also draws on Michelle G. Craske et al., “Maximizing exposure
  therapy: An inhibitory learning approach,” *Behaviour Research and Therapy*
  58, 2014, for learning by facing fear.
- Everything not named above is the author's own.
