# experimentation

Principles for learning through bounded action when reasoning and existing
evidence cannot settle a decision-relevant uncertainty or direction
(`/experimentation`).

## Credit

- Principles 1 and 4 draw on Herminia Ibarra, *Working Identity: Unconventional
  Strategies for Reinventing Your Career*, Harvard Business School Press, 2003,
  for learning possible directions through action and testing several possible
  selves rather than settling identity by introspection.
- Principle 2 draws on Bill Burnett and Dave Evans, *Designing Your Life: How
  to Build a Well-Lived, Joyful Life*, Alfred A. Knopf, 2016, for treating
  observed engagement and energy as clues about what to explore.
- Principle 4 draws on Amy C. Edmondson, “Strategies for Learning from
  Failure,” *Harvard Business Review* 89(4), 2011, for keeping tests at the
  smallest useful scale.
- Principle 5 draws on Saras D. Sarasvathy, “Causation and Effectuation: Toward
  a Theoretical Shift from Economic Inevitability to Entrepreneurial
  Contingency,” *Academy of Management Review* 26(2), 2001, for affordable loss
  rather than hoped-for return.
- The use of these ideas beyond career or life design, Principles 3, 6, and
  7, the remaining content of Principles 1, 2, 4, and 5, and the definitions
  are the author’s own synthesis.
