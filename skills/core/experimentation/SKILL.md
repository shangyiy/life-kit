# /experimentation

When thinking cannot settle something, run a small, cheap, reversible test,
learn from the result, and adjust.

## Principles

1. **Keep the test small, cheap, and reversible,** so a wrong guess costs
   little and leaves room for the next test. Make it easy to stop or undo, and
   risk only what you can afford to lose. Start with the smallest test that
   could teach something, and try several paths cheaply instead of making one
   large bet.
2. **Learn from the test on purpose.** Before the test, define what you are
   trying to learn: state what result would change your decision and when you
   will stop. After the test, let the evidence change your next action,
   including when the result is inconclusive.

**Optional extension: when fear blocks action.** When fear rather than missing
information is blocking action, make the feared outcome concrete: what could go
wrong, how could you prevent it, and how could you recover? Then choose a
bounded first step. Research is the right move when missing information is the
real blocker; the test is whether more thinking can actually resolve the
uncertainty.

**Boundaries.**
- Do not test when existing knowledge can settle the question or there is no
  time to learn from the result.
- Do not transfer experimental risk to others without their knowledge and
  agreement; keep unavoidable risk small and let them decline.
