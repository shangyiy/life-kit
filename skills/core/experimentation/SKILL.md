# /experimentation

The domain of learning through bounded action when reasoning and existing
evidence cannot settle a decision, including which direction deserves
commitment. Where no action is possible, `misfits` studies an existing record
of work instead. Use these principles to do the work or review it. In a review,
name each breach once, with its fix.

## Principles

1. **Experiment when action can change a decision.** Use reasoning, existing
   evidence, or direct inquiry when they can settle the question. Act when an
   important uncertainty remains and the answer could change what is done. If
   the test cannot finish in time, or the choice is one-shot and irreversible,
   decide from the best available evidence. When no candidate direction exists,
   treat what deserves commitment as the uncertainty: follow live questions and
   try small actions rather than waiting for introspection to settle it.
2. **Separate assumptions from evidence.** Confidence, agreement, and
   preference are not observations. State what must be true so evidence can
   support, weaken, or leave it unresolved. Observed engagement and energy
   during action are evidence about where to probe next, not proof that a
   direction is right. Use a baseline and change one thing at a time where
   possible.
3. **Fix the evidence rule before seeing the result.** Decide which
   observations bear on the assumption, what counts as enough evidence, and how
   each possible result changes the decision. Include an inconclusive outcome
   and a stop point. Disclose later revisions instead of fitting the rule to
   the result.
4. **Use the least commitment that can produce useful evidence.** Isolate the
   uncertainty where possible. Before committing to one path, generate several
   contrasting alternatives and give each a cheap probe. Prefer a test that can
   be stopped, undone, or redirected. Increase commitment only when useful
   learning requires it.
5. **Bound exposure by affordable downside, not hoped-for upside.** Set limits
   on losses of resources, time, trust, safety, attention, and alternatives.
   Affordable loss is necessary, not sufficient. A test must cost less than the
   decision it informs is worth.
6. **Respect duties and the people who bear the risk.** Learning does not
   override law, safety, consent, rights, promises, duties, or authorized
   constraints. Identify who benefits and who bears the exposure. Obtain
   required authority or consent. Do not run an unsafe or unlawful action, or
   shift material risk to others because you can afford it.
7. **Keep the result interpretable.** Preserve the conditions needed to
   interpret the result. Calling preventable harm an experiment does not make
   it informative.
8. **Let the evidence alter belief and action.** Record surprises, adverse
   effects, and uncertainty. Draw no stronger conclusion than the observations
   support. Update the assumption and decision. Repeat only to add information.

## Definitions

- **Experiment:** a bounded action chosen to produce evidence about an
  uncertainty.
- **Evidence rule:** a rule fixed before observation that connects possible
  results to the decision each result warrants.
