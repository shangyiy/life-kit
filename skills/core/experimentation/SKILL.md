# /experimentation

The domain of learning through bounded action when reasoning and existing
evidence cannot settle a decision, including which direction deserves
commitment. Where no action is possible, `misfits` studies an existing record
of work instead. Use these principles to do the work or review it. In a review,
name each breach once, with its fix.

## Principles

1. **Experiment when action can change a decision.** Use reasoning, existing
   evidence, or direct inquiry when they can settle the question. Act when an
   important uncertainty remains and the answer could change what is done.
   When no candidate direction exists, follow live questions and try small
   actions rather than expecting introspection alone to settle it. Whether to
   wait or commit belongs to `optionality`.
2. **Separate assumptions from evidence.** Confidence, agreement, and
   preference are not observations. State what must be true so evidence can
   support, weaken, or leave it unresolved. Engagement and energy during action
   guide where to probe next; they do not prove a direction is right. Use a
   baseline and change one thing at a time where possible.
3. **Fix the evidence rule before seeing the result.** For a stated assumption,
   decide which observations bear on it, what counts as enough evidence, and
   how each possible result changes the decision. Include an inconclusive
   outcome and a stop point. For exploration, fix at least the stop point and
   limits in advance. Disclose later revisions instead of fitting the rule to
   the result.
4. **Use the least commitment that can produce useful evidence.** Isolate the
   uncertainty where possible. When several paths are open, generate
   contrasting alternatives and give each a cheap probe before committing.
   Prefer a test that can be stopped, undone, or redirected. Increase
   commitment only when useful learning requires it.
5. **Bound exposure by affordable downside, not hoped-for upside.** Set limits
   on losses of resources, time, trust, safety, attention, and alternatives.
   Affordable loss is necessary, not sufficient. A test must cost less than
   the decision it informs is worth.
6. **Respect duties and the people who bear the risk.** Learning does not
   override law, safety, consent, rights, promises, duties, or authorized
   constraints. Identify who benefits and who bears exposure. Obtain required
   authority or consent. Do not run an unsafe or unlawful action or shift
   material risk to others because you can afford it.
7. **Let the evidence alter belief and action.** Record surprises, adverse
   effects, and uncertainty. Draw no stronger conclusion than the observations
   support. Update the assumption and decision. Repeat only to add information.

## Definitions

- **Experiment:** a bounded action chosen to produce evidence about uncertainty.
- **Evidence rule:** a rule fixed before observation that connects possible
  results to the decision each result warrants.
