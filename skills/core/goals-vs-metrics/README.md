# goals-vs-metrics

Principles for keeping a goal apart from the metric that measures it
(`/goals-vs-metrics`). `SKILL.md` holds the central idea, definitions and
principles.

## Credit

- Charles Goodhart, "Problems of Monetary Management: The U.K. Experience",
  paper from a 1975 conference, published in *Papers in Monetary Economics*
  vol. 1, Reserve Bank of Australia, 1976: a statistical regularity tends to
  collapse once pressure is put on it for control (central idea).
- Donald T. Campbell, "Assessing the impact of planned social change",
  *Evaluation and Program Planning* 2(1), 1979: the more an indicator is used
  for decisions, the more it is pushed to distort what it monitors (central
  idea, principle 4).
- Steven Kerr, "On the Folly of Rewarding A, While Hoping for B", *Academy of
  Management Journal* 18(4), 1975: reward systems often pay for one behavior
  while the rewarder hopes for another (principle 2).
- Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, consent order against Wells Fargo
  Bank, N.A., 2016: sales targets and incentive pay were followed by
  employees opening accounts customers had not authorized (principle 2).
- Jerry Z. Muller, *The Tyranny of Metrics*, Princeton University Press,
  2018: metrics work as a complement to judgment, not a replacement
  (principle 4).

## This skill's own readings

The central idea is derived from the mechanism these works describe; none of
them is offered as proof of it. Most principles follow from it. These parts go
beyond the works above:

- Principle 1: the means-for-end half of the idea, that wanting a means is
  fine, and the step when no end can be named.
- Principle 3: the warning signs and the check close to the goal.
- Principle 4: the advice for a mandated number.

This skill is an independent restatement. It is not affiliated with or
endorsed by any of these authors.
