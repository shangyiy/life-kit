# goals-vs-metrics

Principles for keeping a goal apart from the metric that measures it
(`/goals-vs-metrics`). `SKILL.md` holds the central idea, definitions and
principles.

## Credit

- "打工人如何獲得財富自由？ | Multiple-FIRE 系列", interview with Leon,
  课代表立正, YouTube, 2021: money and promotion are tools, not ends; metrics
  measure strategy and are not the goal (the self-directed split).
- Charles Goodhart, "Problems of Monetary Management: The U.K. Experience",
  *Papers in Monetary Economics* vol. 1, Reserve Bank of Australia, 1975, and
  Marilyn Strathern, "'Improving ratings': audit in the British university
  system", *European Review* 5(3), 1997: a measure used for control stops
  tracking what it measured (principle 2).
- Donald T. Campbell, "Assessing the impact of planned social change",
  *Evaluation and Program Planning* 2(1), 1979: the more an indicator is used
  for decisions, the more it is pushed to distort what it monitors
  (principles 2 and 5).
- Steven Kerr, "On the Folly of Rewarding A, While Hoping for B", *Academy of
  Management Journal* 18(4), 1975: reward systems that pay for one behavior
  while the rewarder hopes for another (principle 3).
- Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, consent order against Wells Fargo
  Bank, N.A., 2016: sales targets and incentive pay were followed by
  employees opening accounts customers had not authorized (principle 3).
- Michael G. Vann, "Of Rats, Rice, and Race: The Great Hanoi Rat Massacre, an
  Episode in French Colonial History", *French Colonial History* 4, 2003: a
  1902 bounty paid per rat tail was followed by tail-cutting and rat
  breeding (principle 3).
- Jerry Z. Muller, *The Tyranny of Metrics*, Princeton University Press,
  2018: gaming and distortion from measured performance; metrics work as a
  complement to judgment, not a replacement (principles 4 and 5).

The central idea is derived from the mechanism these works describe; none of
them is offered as proof of it. Principle 4's note on self-flattery and the
limits in principle 5 are this skill's own reading.

This skill is an independent restatement. It is not affiliated with or
endorsed by any of these authors.
