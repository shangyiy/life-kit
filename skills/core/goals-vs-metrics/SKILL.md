# /goals-vs-metrics

The domain of goals and the metrics that stand in for them, whoever chases the
metric: you, or the people you reward for it. Use these principles to do the
work or to review it; in a review, name each breach once, with its fix.

## Central idea

A metric measures how a means is going, and so stands in for the goal. It tends
to track the goal until effort or reward is aimed at it. The split happens in
two ways: you take a means for the end, or others rewarded on the metric
optimize it, honestly or not. Either way, effort tends to go where the number
moves most cheaply, which is often not where the goal moves, and the more the
number is used for rewards and decisions, the further the two tend to drift
apart. Most principles below follow from this.

## Principles

1. **State the goal without naming a means or a metric.** Whatever you pursue
   to reach an end is a means. Wanting a means is fine while it serves the
   end. If you cannot name an end beyond it, find out whether it serves one
   before you push harder.
2. **Look at what is paid for, not what you hope for.** People tend to do what
   a reward pays for, often at the expense of what is only hoped for.
3. **Watch for the split between metric and goal.** Warning signs: the number
   rises while the goal stands still, effort moves to what is counted, or the
   number is faked. Judge the goal itself, with a check close to it that the
   metric's own effort cannot easily raise.
4. **Keep heavy stakes off the metric.** Use it to learn and to diagnose; tie
   rewards or big decisions to it only as far as you can check the goal behind
   it. Where a number is mandated, report it, use it as an aid to judgment, and
   say openly where it and the goal part.

## Definitions

- **Goal:** what you actually want; an end.
- **Means:** anything pursued to reach the goal, including a chosen plan.
- **Metric:** a visible measure of how a means is going.
