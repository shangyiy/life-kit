# /goals-vs-metrics

The domain of goals and the metrics that stand in for them, whoever chases the
metric: you, or the people you reward for it. Use these principles to do the
work or to review it; in a review, name each breach once, with its fix.

## Central idea

A metric is a stand-in for a goal. It tracks the goal only while nothing
pushes on it. Once effort or reward is aimed at the stand-in, effort goes
wherever the number moves most cheaply, and that is often not where the goal
moves. The number rises and the goal does not. Every principle below follows
from this.

## Definitions

- **Goal:** what you actually want; an end.
- **Strategy:** the means you chose to reach the goal.
- **Metric:** a visible measure of progress on the strategy, used because the
  goal itself cannot be seen directly.

The split happens in two ways. You take a means for an end (money, rank,
score), or others rewarded on the metric optimize it, honestly or not.

## Principles

1. **State the goal without naming any metric.** Money, promotion, praise and
   scores are tools. If the number could reach its maximum while you still
   fail, then you named the metric, not the goal.
2. **Treat every metric as a proxy with a limited life.** The more decisions
   and rewards hang on it, the more pressure it is under, and the weaker its
   link to the goal becomes.
3. **Look at what is paid for, not what you hope for.** People do what the
   reward pays for. A reward on one behavior produces it, even when you hoped
   for another. For how people are measured and rewarded, see `investing` 4
   and `perspective` 1.
4. **Watch for the split between metric and goal.** Warning signs: the number
   rises while the goal stands still, effort moves to what is counted, or the
   number is faked. Read several measures together and judge the goal itself.
   A claim that you chase the end, not the means, can be self-flattery; check
   it against results.
5. **Keep heavy stakes off the metric.** Use a metric to learn and to diagnose.
   Tie rewards or big decisions to it only as far as you can check the goal
   behind it. Metrics are still needed, for shared standards and for practical
   limits such as pay. The fix is to use them as aids to judgment, not as a
   replacement for it.
