# /purpose

Aim the work at its purpose; means and numbers serve the purpose and must not
replace it.

Work drifts toward what is easy to finish or easy to count, and the reason it
exists gets lost. Holding the purpose keeps effort on the change the work is
meant to produce.

## Principles

1. **State the purpose plainly.** Name who benefits and how the work helps
   them, in one sentence that says what should change for them, not how or
   how much.
2. **Own the result.** Answer for the wanted change, not only for finishing
   the assigned work.
3. **Judge every means by the purpose.** Replace a means when it stops
   producing the wanted change or a better one appears; keep the purpose.
4. **Treat completed work and numbers as evidence, not the result.** Completed
   work shows only that a means was used, and a number only that it moved.
   When you cannot see the wanted change itself, say what evidence would show
   it and who can see it.
5. **Expect a chased number to drift.** When decisions depend on it, effort
   goes to the easiest way to raise it, and a reward tied to it speeds this up.
   Ask what the reward encourages, such as neglected quality or costs pushed
   onto people the number leaves out. Trust the number only while direct
   evidence of the wanted change agrees.
6. **Change the purpose openly.** State the old purpose, the new one, and why.
   When a request would change the purpose, say so before acting on it. Don't
   report a different result as if it met the original purpose.

## Definitions

- **Purpose:** the change the work is meant to produce for the people it
  serves.
- **Means:** a way of producing that change.
- **Number:** a measure, target, or count that reports on the work.
