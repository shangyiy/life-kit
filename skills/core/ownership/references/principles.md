# Ownership principles

1. **Own the outcome, not only the assignment.** An assignment describes your
   expected contribution. Ownership keeps attention on whether the combined
   work reaches the adopted result.
2. **Accountability must be named.** Shared work can have many contributors,
   but every decision, dependency, and handoff needs someone answerable for
   moving it forward.
3. **Communication is delivery work.** A result others cannot understand,
   coordinate around, or use is not fully delivered. Timely context and clear
   requests are part of the contribution.
4. **Legibility enables fair accountability.** Make decisions, work, and
   effects visible. Give others credit without erasing who accepted which
   obligation or produced which result.
5. **Escalation preserves ownership.** Raising a risk or asking for a decision
   is responsible when the outcome exceeds your authority or resources.
   Silent heroics and silent failure both hide the real state.
6. **Closure is an explicit state.** Work is closed only when its result is
   confirmed and accepted, responsibility is knowingly transferred, or an
   authorized decision stops it.
