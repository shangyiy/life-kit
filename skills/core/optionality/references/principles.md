# Optionality principles

1. **An option is a feasible future choice.** A possibility is not an option
   unless the decision-maker can actually take it under known conditions.
   More credible alternatives reduce dependence on any one counterpart or
   course and make walking away real.
2. **A way out must survive contact with the risk.** Name its holder, evidence,
   stated terms, duration, and whether the event that creates the need would
   also destroy the fallback. Missing or conflicting facts make it a claim,
   not protection.
3. **Coverage is part of credibility.** A fallback counts only through the
   whole period and portion of exposure it covers. Decide uncovered exposure
   as if no way out existed.
4. **Options have costs and clocks.** Maintaining, exercising, and delaying a
   choice consume resources or other opportunities. Waiting can add
   information, but can also erode the option or the value it could capture.
5. **Opportunity density matters more than option count.** Environments differ
   in how often reachable people, information, resources, and adjacent paths
   produce real alternatives. Judge accessibility and quality, not abundance
   in name only.
6. **Optionality serves commitment.** Flexibility is valuable under
   uncertainty and asymmetric downside. Commitment is valuable when focus,
   trust, or compounding requires it. The aim is not maximum choice, but enough
   credible choice to commit without being trapped.
