# optionality

Principles for judging the credible choices a decision preserves, creates,
spends, or closes, with only the definitions they require (`/optionality`).

## Credit

- Roger Fisher, William Ury, and Bruce Patton, *Getting to Yes: Negotiating
  Agreement Without Giving In*, Penguin Books, 2011
  ([publisher](https://penguinrandomhousehighereducation.com/book/?isbn=9780143118756)).
- Avinash K. Dixit and Robert S. Pindyck, *Investment under Uncertainty*,
  Princeton University Press, 1994
  ([publisher](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691034102/investment-under-uncertainty)).
- Leon, “打工人如何获得财富自由？｜什么才是真正的财富和真正的自由？（中文字幕）｜
  Multiple-Fire系列,” 课代表立正, August 6, 2021
  ([video](https://youtu.be/9LKJ8JdLtfI)).
- Leon, “如何摆脱无意义的工作？｜Multiple-Fire系列,” 课代表立正, May 23,
  2023 ([video](https://youtu.be/dlv97OHFnGY)).
- Sim B. Sitkin, Kelly E. See, C. Chet Miller, Michael W. Lawless, and Andrew
  M. Carton, “The Paradox of Stretch Goals: Organizations in Pursuit of the
  Seemingly Impossible,” *Academy of Management Review*, 36(3), 2011, 544–566
  ([article](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.2008.0038)).

Source-to-principle map:

- Fisher, Ury, and Patton inform principle 1: credible alternatives and the
  capacity to walk away.
- Dixit and Pindyck inform principle 5's value lost while waiting and
  principle 6: the value of waiting under uncertainty before an irreversible
  commitment.
- Leon's 2021 talk informs principle 1's walk-away threshold. His 2023 talk
  informs principle 5's treatment of choices that lose value with time.
- Sitkin et al. inform principle 8's slack clause: slack lets failure of a
  stretch goal be absorbed. The point about replacing people or teams is the
  author's own.

Everything else, including the opener, the definitions, and the remaining
rules of principles 1–8, is the author's own.
