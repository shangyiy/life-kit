# optionality

Principles for checking that a way out is real before relying on it
(`/optionality`).

## Credit

- Roger Fisher, William Ury, and Bruce Patton, *Getting to Yes: Negotiating
  Agreement Without Giving In*, Penguin Books, 2011
  ([publisher](https://penguinrandomhousehighereducation.com/book/?isbn=9780143118756)):
  principle 1's rule to count only a credible alternative.
- Avinash K. Dixit and Robert S. Pindyck, *Investment under Uncertainty*,
  Princeton University Press, 1994
  ([publisher](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691034102/investment-under-uncertainty)):
  principle 3's value of waiting for useful information before a choice closes.
- Leon, “如何摆脱无意义的工作？｜Multiple-Fire系列,” 课代表立正, May 23,
  2023 ([video](https://youtu.be/dlv97OHFnGY)): principle 3's warning that a
  choice can lose value with time.
- Sim B. Sitkin, Kelly E. See, C. Chet Miller, Michael W. Lawless, and Andrew
  M. Carton, “The Paradox of Stretch Goals: Organizations in Pursuit of the
  Seemingly Impossible,” *Academy of Management Review*, 36(3), 2011, 544–566
  ([article](https://doi.org/10.5465/amr.2008.0038)): principle 4's rule that
  slack must be enough to absorb a miss.

Principle 1's holder, terms, and proof checks; principle 2; principle 4's
survivable-miss wording; the workflow, checklist, anti-patterns, output, and
skill pointers are the author's own.

endorsed by any of these authors or speakers.
