# optionality

Find the real choices you have and judge what each is worth
(`/optionality`).

## Example

This example only shows the idea; the skill itself holds the principles.
Take moving abroad for work versus staying in your home country.

- **Upside and your ability to capture it.** Moving may open bigger
  opportunities. Ask whether you can reach, recognize, and qualify for them,
  and whether your skills, savings, and contacts are enough to act. Staying
  has its own upside, and your odds there may be better or worse.
- **Downside and the cost of keeping each path open.** Moving can cost savings,
  relationships, and a stalled career back home. Keeping both paths open costs
  money, time, and attention, and it is not free.
- **Whether you can come back.** If you can return, the downside is bounded and
  moving is closer to a choice. If a contract or other future obligation traps
  you, it is a bet.
- **Time windows.** An offer, a job opening, or a stage of life can close.

The point: do not pick one path now. Build a way to keep both open. Take small
steps to learn more, such as a short visit, a trial period, or talking to
people who have done it. Build up savings and skills that raise your ceiling on
either path. Then decide before the window closes, since waiting is also a
choice.
