# /needs-to-signals

Translate what an audience needs into observable signals that show what
matters to its decisions.

A need is what the audience must see addressed in order to decide. It may
arrive as a job description, an admissions prompt, what a customer says in
an interview, or any other statement of what matters to them.

This skill defines what should be demonstrated, not whether it has been
demonstrated. Evaluation and verdicts belong to the consuming skill.

## Principles

1. **Understand and translate needs.** Start from the audience's goals,
   constraints, and decision. Use `perspective` when needed, reusing
   existing models and skipping it when needs are explicit. Translate
   needs into required outcomes rather than preferred solutions, while
   preserving consequential hard requirements.
2. **Make signals observable.** Identify concrete capabilities, behaviors,
   decisions, or outcomes that demonstrate a material need. Do not force
   signals into metrics; reasoning, judgment, methods, and results can
   all be observable.
3. **Preserve signal integrity.** Distinguish signals from evidence, and
   explicit needs from supported inferences and unknowns. Never turn
   unknowns into requirements or treat evidence as proof that a signal
   is met.
