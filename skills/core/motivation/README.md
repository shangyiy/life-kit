# motivation

Judge whether a motive can keep a person acting by asking whether they would
still do it without the outside reward or pressure (`/motivation`). It draws on
work on intrinsic and extrinsic motivation, self-efficacy, goals, and
curiosity.

## Credit

- Richard M. Ryan and Edward L. Deci, “Self-Determination Theory and the
  Facilitation of Intrinsic Motivation, Social Development, and Well-Being,”
  *American Psychologist* 55(1), 2000: intrinsic and extrinsic motivation,
  internalization, guilt and self-pressure as a partly internalized motive,
  autonomy, competence, relatedness, pressure and controlling evaluation, and
  support through choice.
- Albert Bandura, “Self-Efficacy: Toward a Unifying Theory of Behavioral
  Change,” *Psychological Review* 84(2), 1977: past performance, seeing
  others succeed, persuasion, and physical or emotional state as sources of
  self-efficacy.
- Edward L. Deci, Richard Koestner, and Richard M. Ryan,
  “A Meta-Analytic Review of Experiments Examining the Effects of Extrinsic
  Rewards on Intrinsic Motivation,” *Psychological Bulletin* 125(6), 1999:
  how expected material rewards can reduce existing interest, while unexpected
  rewards and positive feedback did not.
- Edwin A. Locke and Gary P. Latham, “Building a Practically Useful Theory of
  Goal Setting and Task Motivation: A 35-Year Odyssey,” *American
  Psychologist* 57(9), 2002: clear, demanding goals matched to ability, and
  feedback.
- George Loewenstein, “The Psychology of Curiosity: A Review and
  Reinterpretation,” *Psychological Bulletin* 116(1), 1994: curiosity as a
  wish to close a gap in what one knows.
- The author's own ideas, not taken from the sources above: the opening
  question, that the person's answer shows more than their story, asking what
  else a needed task gives, that comparison with similar people can start a
  motive, that putting a task off can come from doubt, that curiosity can start
  interest, that curiosity and structure do not replace a reason the person
  holds, the boundaries on safety, steering, neutral advice and tasks others
  depend on, and how to help someone helping another person.
