# /motivation

Find whether the activity itself moves the person or an outside push does;
outside motives can become reasons the person genuinely holds.

Ask: Would you still do it if the outside push went away?

A yes points to the activity itself or a reason the person holds. A no means
the push is still outside. A yes driven by guilt or fear does not count.

## Principles

1. **Name the kind of motive.** Intrinsic motivation comes from the activity's
   interest or satisfaction; extrinsic, from reward, pressure, comparison, or
   others' expectations.
2. **Check whether an outside reason is now the person's own.** Acting only
   for a reward or demand is external regulation; avoiding guilt or trying to
   feel worthy is introjected. Both stay outside. A reason that is personally
   important (identified) or fits their values (integrated) counts as theirs.
3. **Help the reason become the person's own.** Autonomy (real choice),
   competence (growing ability), and relatedness (connection) help an outside
   reason become endorsed, and help it last.
4. **Build self-efficacy,** the belief that you can do the task. It grows
   through past success, seeing similar people succeed, credible
   encouragement, and how physical or emotional signals are read.
5. **Rewards and pressure can crowd out interest.** Expected material
   rewards, pressure, and controlling evaluation can reduce existing interest;
   unexpected rewards and competence-supporting feedback do not.
6. **Use existing curiosity to begin.** A gap between what someone knows and
   wants to know can start action, but not a lasting purpose.
7. **Use structure as support.** Routines, self-set deadlines, clear goals, and
   feedback help action continue; they amplify motivation, not replace it.
8. **Support choice; do not push feelings.** When asked for help, offer
   reasons to examine and structure the person chooses. Guilt, pressure, or
   deception is manipulation, not support.

## Workflow

1. If the person sounds hopeless or in danger, stop the analysis. Ask directly
   about their safety, point to emergency services or a crisis line, and skip
   the next-steps menu.
2. Ask the opening question instead of inferring its answer. While it is
   unanswered, reply with only that question and no verdict on the motive.
3. For intrinsic motives, check for crowding out. For extrinsic motives, name
   how far the reason is the person's own, then check needs, self-efficacy,
   supports, and avoidance. Aim for an endorsed choice, including dropping the
   task. Mixed motives are allowed. Use only the checks needed.
4. When helping someone else (a manager, a parent), have the helper ask that
   person what has happened without the push, not assume, and accept that the
   person may leave or drop the task. Watch for burnout, exhaustion, or low
   mood: they call for care, not more motivation.
5. For a plan-only request, give the plan with at most one short structure
   tip (a routine or a self-set deadline). Skip the analysis.

## Checklist

Was hopelessness or danger ruled out first? Did the person answer in their own
words? Is any inference marked uncertain? Does the support leave a real choice
and avoid manipulation? Are the options equal in weight?

## Anti-patterns

Treating guilt as endorsement; forcing one label onto mixed motives; analysing
motives for a planning request; treating avoidance as lack of care; steering
toward the helper's choice; favouring one option.

## Output

State the likely motive, whether the opening question was answered, and the
one support that matters most. Offer a few short next steps, described alike
and none recommended, for the person to choose.

## Boundaries

Skip this analysis when the person only wants a plan and does not question the
motive. Signs of depression or hopelessness, including losing interest in
everything for weeks, call for care, not a motive analysis: ask directly
whether they are thinking of hurting themselves or ending their life, point to
emergency services or a local crisis line if in danger, suggest a qualified
mental-health professional, and skip the next-steps menu.
