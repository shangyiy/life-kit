# goal-design

Principles for choosing which goals to adopt, why they matter, what they may
cost, and when to reconsider them (`/goal-design`).

## Credit

1. **Adoption rather than inheritance; source distinct from reason
   (Principles 1–2):** Edward L. Deci and Richard M. Ryan, “The ‘What’ and
   ‘Why’ of Goal Pursuits: Human Needs and the Self-Determination of
   Behavior,” *Psychological Inquiry* 11(4), 2000
   ([article](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15327965PLI1104_01)), for the
   distinction between externally regulated conduct and conduct accepted as
   one's own.
2. **A goal connected to the chooser's values (Principle 2):** Kennon M.
   Sheldon and Andrew J. Elliot, “Goal Striving, Need Satisfaction, and
   Longitudinal Well-Being: The Self-Concordance Model,” *Journal of
   Personality and Social Psychology* 76(3), 1999
   ([article](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.76.3.482)), for
   self-concordant goals arising from a person's interests and values.

The binding-duty and joint-goal qualifications in Principle 1 are
author-created. Principles 3–8—the available choices and their consequences,
commitment bounds, exploratory probes, opportunity-cost comparison,
present-choice review, changed-facts threshold, treatment of spent effort,
and non-rejectable constraints—are author-created and have no external
source.
