# goal-design

Principles for choosing which goals to adopt, why they matter, what they may
cost, and when to reconsider them (`/goal-design`).

## Credit

1. **A goal is adopted rather than inherited (Principle 1):** Edward L. Deci
   and Richard M. Ryan, “The ‘What’ and ‘Why’ of Goal Pursuits: Human Needs
   and the Self-Determination of Behavior,” *Psychological Inquiry* 11(4),
   2000 ([article](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15327965PLI1104_01)), for the
   difference between externally regulated conduct and conduct accepted as
   one's own.
2. **A goal connected to the chooser's values (Principle 2):** Kennon M.
   Sheldon and Andrew J. Elliot, “Goal Striving, Need Satisfaction, and
   Longitudinal Well-Being: The Self-Concordance Model,” *Journal of
   Personality and Social Psychology* 76(3), 1999
   ([article](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.76.3.482)), for
   self-concordant goals arising from a person's interests and values.

The author's own: the binding-duty and joint-goal qualifications in Principle
1, keeping a goal's source apart from the reason for adopting it, and the
framing of Principles 3–7. Principles 5 and 6 restate the common ideas of
opportunity cost and of ignoring sunk cost, which the skill does not take
from any one source.

This skill is an independent restatement. It is not affiliated with or
endorsed by any of these authors.
