# Goal-design principles

1. **A goal is adopted, not inherited.** External expectations are inputs to a
   decision. They do not become commitments until the person who must pursue
   them accepts the commitment.
2. **The reason belongs beside the goal.** A goal worth pursuing connects to
   an end the chooser values. External rewards can support that end, but they
   cannot establish personal importance by themselves.
3. **Assignment creates four legitimate choices.** Accept the goal as stated,
   renegotiate its result or cost, defer it, or reject it. Silence and motion
   are not substitutes for choosing.
4. **Success and failure bound the commitment.** Success names the change
   sought; failure names nonattainment or unacceptable harm. Limits state
   what the goal may not consume or damage.
5. **Every goal competes.** Time, attention, money, and risk spent here cannot
   serve the next-best goal. A goal is justified against those alternatives,
   not in isolation.
6. **Commitment is conditional on reality.** Persist through ordinary
   difficulty, but revisit the goal when its reason, assumptions, costs, or
   surrounding priorities change. Past effort is not a reason to continue.
