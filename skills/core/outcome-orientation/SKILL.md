# /outcome-orientation

The domain of translating an adopted reason into the useful change work should
produce, then keeping methods and measures answerable to that change. The
person's reason for acting comes from `motivation`; this skill does not supply
or manufacture it. Carrying an adopted result through delivery, communication,
and closure belongs to `ownership`. Binding duties and constraints still
apply; only an authorized party can change them. Use these principles to do
the work or review it; in a review, name each breach once, with its fix.

## Principles

1. **Turn the adopted why into an end.** Name who the work serves and the
   useful change that would count as success. Confirm that this result follows
   from the reason established through `motivation`. This result is the end.
   Scale the effort to the stakes.
2. **Judge work by its effects.** Effort, speed, and completion show activity,
   not value. Examine what changed for those affected, whether it serves the
   result, and what material side effects it had. When direct observation is
   not practical, use the strongest available evidence and state its limits.
3. **Hold the result while changing the method.** Change a method when its link
   to the result fails. Change or drop the result only openly when evidence,
   constraints, or unacceptable effects undermine it. State what changed and
   who decided. Never present an easier substitute as the original result
   attained.
4. **Make trade-offs explicit.** When scope, speed, quality, cost, and risk
   cannot all improve together, state their order and the consequence of the
   choice. Use `ownership` for accepting, communicating, changing, handing
   off, and closing the resulting commitment.
5. **Never turn a means into the end.** Treating a metric, deliverable,
   activity, reward, rating, or promotion as the end is the inversion failure.
   Measures are evidence and incentives are levers, not the result. A measure
   is useful only while its link to the result holds; pressure, rewards, and
   sanctions can weaken that link. Inspect the result, use more than one kind
   of evidence when the stakes warrant it, and check what conduct an incentive
   makes attractive, including gaming, neglected quality, and costs shifted to
   people the measure does not represent.

## Definitions

- **Result:** the useful change the work is intended to produce.
- **Means:** anything used to pursue, observe, or reward a result.
- **Measure:** an observation or proxy used as evidence about a result.
