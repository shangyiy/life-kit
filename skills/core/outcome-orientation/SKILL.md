# /outcome-orientation

The domain of outcome orientation: keeping work answerable to the useful
change it is meant to produce while respecting the duties and constraints
that govern it. Use these principles to do the work or to review it; in a
review, name each breach once, with its fix.

## Principles

1. **Name the intended result and why it matters.** State the useful change
   that would constitute success. Keep the result distinct from the activity,
   deliverable, method, or measure used to pursue or observe it. A required
   deliverable may satisfy a duty without proving that a broader result
   occurred.
2. **Judge work by its effects.** Effort, speed, and completion are evidence
   of activity, not sufficient evidence of value. Examine what changed for
   those affected, whether the change serves the intended result, and what
   material effects occurred beyond it. Where direct observation is
   impracticable, use the strongest available evidence and state its limits.
3. **Keep the result stable and the method revisable.** Prefer changing a
   method whose credible link to the result has failed over quietly changing
   the result. A prescribed method remains binding when a legitimate duty or
   constraint requires it; make the resulting limit on adaptation explicit.
4. **Make trade-offs explicit.** When scope, speed, quality, cost, and risk
   cannot all be improved together, state their order and the consequence of
   the choice. Treat binding duties and constraints as boundaries rather than
   discretionary trade-offs unless an authorized party changes them.
5. **Use measures as evidence, not substitutes for the result.** A measure
   can illuminate progress while its relation to the result remains credible.
   Pressure to improve it can weaken that relation, especially when rewards
   or sanctions depend on it. Inspect the underlying result, use more than
   one relevant kind of evidence where the stakes warrant it, and qualify
   conclusions when the relation cannot be checked.
6. **Align incentives with the intended result and its safeguards.** Rewarding
   an activity or measure can produce that target while displacing the result
   it was meant to serve. Examine what conduct the incentive makes attractive,
   including gaming, neglected quality, shifted costs, and harm to those not
   represented by the target. No incentive overrides a binding duty.
7. **Change the result openly when its basis no longer holds.** New evidence,
   changed constraints, conflicting duties, or unacceptable effects can
   warrant revision, suspension, or abandonment. Record what changed, who is
   authorized to decide, and the new result or disposition; do not present an
   easier substitute as attainment of the original result.

## Definitions

- **Result:** the useful change the work is intended to produce.
- **Method:** an activity, plan, or deliverable used to pursue a result.
- **Measure:** an observation or proxy used as evidence about a result.
- **Binding duty or constraint:** a requirement that the work must satisfy
  unless a party authorized to change it does so.
