# Outcome-orientation principles

1. **Understand why before deciding how.** The objective is the useful change
   sought. A task, deliverable, plan, or metric is a means and must remain
   answerable to that change.
2. **Judge work by effect, not motion.** Effort, speed, and completion do not
   establish value. Ask what changed for the intended beneficiary and whether
   that change serves the objective.
3. **Keep the objective stable and the method revisable.** Commitment to a
   result does not require loyalty to the first implementation. Evidence can
   invalidate a method without invalidating the objective.
4. **Make trade-offs explicit.** Limited resources force choices among scope,
   speed, quality, cost, and risk. Choose and communicate the order instead of
   letting the method decide it silently.
5. **A metric is evidence, not the goal.** Pressure on a proxy can separate it
   from the result it once tracked. Check the result itself and keep high
   stakes off a metric when that link cannot be verified.
6. **Renegotiate impossible outcomes openly.** Constraints may require a new
   objective. Naming that change preserves accountability; quietly delivering
   an easier result does not.
