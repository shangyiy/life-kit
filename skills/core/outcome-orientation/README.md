# outcome-orientation

Principles for keeping work answerable to its intended result
(`/outcome-orientation`).

## Credit

- **Principle 5:** Charles A. E. Goodhart, “Problems of Monetary Management:
  The U.K. Experience,” *Papers in Monetary Economics*, Reserve Bank of
  Australia, 1976; Donald T. Campbell, “Assessing the Impact of Planned Social
  Change,” *Evaluation and Program Planning* 2(1), 1979; and Jerry Z. Muller,
  *The Tyranny of Metrics*, Princeton University Press, 2018. These works
  support the limits of pressured measures and the use of metrics as aids to
  judgment.
- **Principle 6:** Donald T. Campbell, “Assessing the Impact of Planned Social
  Change,” *Evaluation and Program Planning* 2(1), 1979; Steven Kerr, “On the
  Folly of Rewarding A, While Hoping for B,” *Academy of Management Journal*
  18(4), 1975; and Jerry Z. Muller, *The Tyranny of Metrics*, Princeton
  University Press, 2018. These works support the risks of distorted conduct,
  displaced purposes, and rewards aimed at substitutes for the desired result.
- **Author-created principles without literature sources:** Principles 1–4
  and 7.

This skill is an independent restatement. It is not affiliated with or
endorsed by any of these authors.
