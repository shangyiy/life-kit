# outcome-orientation

Judge work by the real result, not completed activity or a proxy that stands
in for it (`/outcome-orientation`). This skill incorporates the core of the
former goals-versus-metrics draft.

## Credit

- Charles A. E. Goodhart, “Problems of Monetary Management: The U.K.
  Experience,” *Papers in Monetary Economics*, Reserve Bank of Australia,
  1976 ([bibliography](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/rdp/1990/9013/conference-volumes.html)).
- Donald T. Campbell, “Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change,”
  *Evaluation and Program Planning* 2(1), 1979
  ([article](https://doi.org/10.1016/0149-7189(79)90048-X)).
- Steven Kerr, “On the Folly of Rewarding A, While Hoping for B,” *Academy of
  Management Journal* 18(4), 1975
  ([article](https://doi.org/10.2307/255378)).
- Jerry Z. Muller, *The Tyranny of Metrics*, Princeton University Press, 2018
  ([publisher](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691174952/the-tyranny-of-metrics)).

These works support the risk that targets and rewards separate a measure from
the desired result, and the use of metrics as aids to judgment. The broader
result-method distinction, trade-off rules, and workflow are this skill's own
synthesis.

This skill is an independent restatement. It is not affiliated with or
endorsed by any of these authors.
