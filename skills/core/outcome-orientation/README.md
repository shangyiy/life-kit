# outcome-orientation

Principles for keeping work answerable to why it exists
(`/outcome-orientation`).

## Credit

- Leon, “打工人如何获得财富自由？｜什么才是真正的财富和真正的自由？（中文字幕）｜Multiple-Fire系列,” guest on
  课代表立正, 2021-08-06, https://youtu.be/9LKJ8JdLtfI: Principle 1's
  ends-before-means spine and Principle 5's inversion failure. He distinguishes ends
  from money, promotion, ratings, rewards, processes, and measures, and says
  that making a measure the goal distorts action.
- Charles A. E. Goodhart, “Problems of Monetary Management: The U.K.
  Experience,” *Papers in Monetary Economics*, Reserve Bank of Australia,
  1976 (conference held 1975), and Donald T. Campbell, “Assessing the Impact of Planned Social
  Change,” *Evaluation and Program Planning* 2(1), 1979: a measure loses its
  link to the result once pressure is put on it. Supports Principle 5.
- Steven Kerr, “On the Folly of Rewarding A, While Hoping for B,” *Academy of
  Management Journal* 18(4), 1975: rewarding a substitute produces the
  substitute and displaces the result. Supports Principle 5.
- Jerry Z. Muller, *The Tyranny of Metrics*, Princeton University Press, 2018:
  measures as aids to judgment, not replacements for it. Supports Principle
  5.

The remaining principles and language are the author's own.

This skill is an independent restatement. It is not affiliated with or
endorsed by any of these authors or speakers.
