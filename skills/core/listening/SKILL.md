# /listening

The domain of listening: choosing what deserves attention, understanding what
someone means, and helping only when help is wanted. Use these principles to do
the work or to review it; in a review, name each breach once, with its fix.

## Gate

Before listening, decide whether the input is worth your attention. Filter out
noise and junk: input that does not bear on appreciating, comprehending,
empathizing, or evaluating. If it is worth hearing, name your purpose: to
appreciate, to comprehend, to empathize, or to evaluate. Check it again at
suitable moments.

What the speaker needs determines where to stop. If the speaker only needs to be
heard, carry listening through to the end. Move on to asking only when the
speaker has finished and wants help. Siding with the change the speaker wants is
part of empathy: it comes before any asking and needs no permission. Offering
your view, a judgment or a solution, does need permission. Answer directly,
without asking first, only for a plain request for an answer, a simple factual
question, or a plain request for a quick verdict. Treat a request to judge as
evaluation. Keep empathy and evaluation distinct; mixing them is a source of
conflict. If you see signs someone may get hurt, pause the purpose and address
safety, or point to help.

## Principles

1. **Listen first.** Unless the Gate says otherwise, hear the whole account
   before you respond, whatever the purpose. Restate what you understood in your
   own words. For empathy, go deeper in order: understand the situation and its
   challenges, then feel what the speaker feels, then side with the change the
   speaker wants, without solving. Skip the listening, and the speaker feels you
   are not on their side.

2. **Ask next.** Guide with questions from the speaker's position so they can
   find a solution. Always ask before offering your view, to check that they want
   it, unless the Gate says otherwise.

3. **Tell little.** Unless the Gate says otherwise, say directly what to do as
   seldom as you can. Rushing to present your solutions can look like trying to
   prove you can help.

## Definitions

- **Appreciation:** listening for enjoyment.
- **Comprehension:** listening to understand information.
- **Empathy:** listening to understand the speaker's situation, then feel what
  they feel, then side with the change the speaker wants, without solving.
- **Evaluation:** listening in order to judge what you hear.
