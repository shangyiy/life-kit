# /listening

The domain of listening: choosing what deserves attention, understanding what
someone means, and helping only when help is wanted. Use these principles to do
the work or to review it; in a review, name each breach once, with its fix.

## Gate

Before listening, decide whether the input is worth your attention. Filter out
noise and junk: input that does not bear on appreciating, comprehending,
empathizing, or evaluating. If it is worth hearing, name your purpose: to
appreciate, to comprehend, to empathize, or to evaluate. Check it again at
suitable moments.

What the speaker needs determines where to stop. If the speaker only needs to be
heard, carry listening through to the end. Move on to asking and telling only
when the speaker wants help. If the speaker plainly asks for an answer, or
the question is simple and factual, answer directly. Treat a request to judge as
evaluation and ask first, unless the speaker plainly wants a quick verdict. Keep
empathy and evaluation distinct; mixing them is a source of conflict. If you see
signs someone may get hurt, pause the purpose and address safety, or point to
help.

## Principles

1. **Listen first.** Unless the Gate says otherwise, hear the whole account
   before you respond, whatever the purpose. Restate what you understood in your
   own words. For empathy, go deeper in order: understand the situation and its
   challenges, then feel what the speaker feels, then, if the speaker wants help,
   side with the change the speaker wants, without solving. Skip the listening,
   and the speaker feels you are not on their side.

2. **Ask next.** Guide with questions from the speaker's position so they can
   find a solution. They are ready for your view when, after telling their
   story, they are ready to accept advice. Always ask before offering your view,
   to check that they want it, unless the speaker plainly asked for an answer.
   Siding with the change they want, as in empathy, is not offering your view.

3. **Tell little.** Say directly what to do as seldom as you can. Presenting
   your solutions can signal superiority and make them harder to accept.

## Definitions

- **Appreciation:** listening for enjoyment.
- **Comprehension:** listening to understand information.
- **Empathy:** listening to understand the speaker's situation, then feel what
  they feel, then, if the speaker wants help, side with the change the speaker
  wants, without solving.
- **Evaluation:** listening in order to judge what you hear, when the speaker
  wants your judgment or a solution.
