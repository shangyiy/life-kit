# /listening

The domain of listening: choosing what deserves attention, understanding what
someone means, and helping only when help is wanted. Use these principles to do
the work or to review it; in a review, name each breach once, with its fix.

## Gate

Before listening, decide whether the input is worth your attention. Filter out
noise and junk. If it is worth hearing, name your purpose: to appreciate, to
comprehend, to empathize, or to evaluate. Check it again at suitable moments.

That purpose determines where to stop. If the speaker only needs to be heard,
carry listening through to the end. Move on to asking and telling only when the
speaker wants a solution. Keep empathy and evaluation distinct; mixing them is
a common source of conflict. If what you hear signals a risk of harm, deal with
that first, whatever the purpose.

## Principles

1. **Listen first.** Hear the whole account before judging. Restate what you
   understood in your own words; nodding does not show what you understood.
   Empathy deepens in order: understand the situation and its challenges, feel
   what the speaker feels, then—if help is wanted—support progress on what the
   speaker wants to change. Skip this, and the speaker feels you are not on
   their side and will not want your view.

2. **Ask next.** Guide with questions from the speaker’s position so they can
   find a solution. They are ready for your view when, after telling their
   story, they open up and ask for it. If you are unsure, ask whether they want
   your view.

3. **Tell as little as possible.** Say directly what to do as seldom as you
   can; guide with questions instead. Presenting your solutions can signal
   superiority and make them harder to accept. The aim is their result, not
   proving that you can help.

## Definitions

- **Appreciation:** listening for enjoyment.
- **Comprehension:** listening to understand information, such as directions.
- **Empathy:** listening to understand the speaker’s situation, feelings, and
  desired change.
- **Evaluation:** listening in order to judge what you hear and hand over a
  solution.
