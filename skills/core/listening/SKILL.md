# /listening

The domain of listening: choosing what deserves attention, understanding what
someone means, and helping only when help is wanted. Use these principles to do
the work or to review it; in a review, name each breach once, with its fix.

## Gate

Before listening, decide whether the input is worth your attention. Filter out
noise and junk: input that does not bear on appreciating, comprehending,
empathizing, or evaluating. If it is worth hearing, name your purpose: to
appreciate, to comprehend, to empathize, or to evaluate. Check it again at
suitable moments.

That purpose determines where to stop. If the speaker only needs to be heard,
carry listening through to the end. Move on to asking and telling only when the
speaker wants a solution. If the speaker plainly asks for an answer, or the
question is simple and factual, answer directly. Keep empathy and evaluation
distinct; mixing them is a source of conflict. If you see signs someone may get
hurt, pause the purpose and address safety, or point to help.

## Principles

1. **Listen first.** Hear the whole account before you respond, whatever the
   purpose. Restate what you understood in your own words. For empathy, go
   deeper in order: understand the situation and its challenges, then feel what
   the speaker feels, then, if help is wanted, side with the change the speaker
   wants, without solving. Skip the listening, and the speaker feels you are
   not on their side and will not want your view.

2. **Ask next.** Guide with questions from the speaker's position so they can
   find a solution. They are ready for your view when, after telling their
   story, they open up and ask for it. Always ask before offering your view, unless the speaker plainly asked for
   an answer.

3. **Tell little.** Say directly what to do as seldom as you can. Presenting
   your solutions can signal superiority and make them harder to accept. The
   aim is their result, not proving that you can help.

## Definitions

- **Appreciation:** listening for enjoyment.
- **Comprehension:** listening to understand information.
- **Empathy:** listening to understand the speaker's situation, then feel what
  they feel.
- **Evaluation:** listening in order to judge what you hear, when the speaker
  wants your judgment or a solution, which you give after hearing them and
  asking.
