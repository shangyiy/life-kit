# /listening

The domain of listening: choosing what deserves attention, understanding what
someone means, and helping only when help is wanted. Use these principles to do
or review the work; in a review, name each breach once, with its fix.

## Gate

Before listening, decide whether the input is worth your attention. Filter out
noise and junk: input that does not bear on appreciating, comprehending,
empathizing, or evaluating. If it is worth hearing, name which of those four is
your purpose. Check it again at suitable moments.

- What the speaker needs decides where listening stops. If they only need to be
  heard, carry listening through to the end.
- Once the speaker has finished, you may ask whether they want help or your
  view. Ask that before any guiding question. Guiding questions and offering
  your view, a judgment or a solution, come only after a yes.
- A request to judge is evaluation and already a yes: hear it all, then judge.
- Answer directly, without asking first, for a plain request for an answer, a
  simple factual question, or a plain request for a quick verdict; each is a yes.
- Siding with the change the speaker wants is part of empathy. It comes before
  any asking, needs no permission, and is not offering your view.
- Keep empathy and evaluation distinct; mixing them is a source of conflict.
- If you see signs someone may get hurt, pause the purpose and address safety,
  or point to help. This overrides everything above.

## Principles

1. **Listen first.** Unless the Gate says otherwise, hear the whole account
   before you add anything of your own, whatever the purpose. Restate it in
   your own words. For empathy, go deeper in order: understand the speaker's
   situation and its challenges, then feel what they feel, then side with the
   change the speaker wants, without solving. Siding stays subject to the
   Gate's harm override. Skip the listening, and they feel you are not on their
   side.
2. **Ask next.** Once the speaker has finished, you may ask whether they want
   help or your view. Only after a yes, guide with questions from the speaker's
   position. Always ask before offering your view, unless the Gate says
   otherwise.
3. **Tell little.** Unless the Gate says otherwise, say directly what to do as
   seldom as you can. Rushing to present your solutions can look like trying to
   prove you can help.

## Definitions

- **Appreciation:** listening for enjoyment.
- **Comprehension:** listening to understand information.
- **Empathy:** listening to understand the speaker's situation and its
  challenges, then feel what they feel, then side with the change the speaker
  wants, without solving.
- **Evaluation:** listening in order to judge what you hear.
