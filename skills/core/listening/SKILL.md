# /listening

The domain of listening: choosing what deserves attention, understanding what
someone means, and helping only when help is wanted. Use these principles to do
or review the work; in a review, name each breach once, with its fix.

## Gate

The Gate decides what to listen to and how to respond. Before listening,
decide whether the input is worth your attention. Filter out noise and junk:
input that does not bear on appreciating, comprehending, empathizing, or
evaluating. If it is worth hearing, name which of those four is your purpose.
Check both again at suitable moments, also after the speaker finishes.

- What the speaker needs decides where listening stops. If they only need to be
  heard, carry listening through to the end.
- Once a speaker has finished, ask whether they want help or your view before
  any guiding question or view. Guiding questions and your view, a judgment or
  a solution, come only after a yes.
- A request to judge or a plain request for an answer already counts as a yes:
  for a judgment, hear it all, then judge; otherwise answer directly. Input
  with no speaker to ask needs no yes to be evaluated.
- Siding with the change the speaker wants needs no permission.
- Keep empathy and evaluation distinct; mixing them is a source of conflict.
- If you see signs someone may get hurt, pause the purpose and address safety,
  or point to help. This overrides everything else.

## Principles

1. **Listen first.** Unless the Gate says otherwise, hear the whole account
   before you add anything of your own, whatever the purpose. Where the purpose
   calls for it, restate it in your own words. For empathy, work through the
   levels in its definition, in order. Skip the listening, and they feel you
   are not on their side.
2. **Ask next.** Once the Gate allows guiding, ask questions from the
   speaker's position.
3. **Tell little.** Unless the Gate says otherwise, say directly what to do as
   seldom as you can. Rushing to present your solutions can look like trying to
   prove you can help.

## Definitions

- **Appreciation:** listening for enjoyment.
- **Comprehension:** listening to understand information.
- **Empathy:** listening to understand the speaker's situation and its
  challenges, then feel what they feel, then side with the change the speaker
  wants, without solving. Siding is part of empathy, not offering your view.
- **Evaluation:** listening in order to judge what you hear.
- **Guiding question:** a question that steers the speaker toward a view or a
  solution. A question asked only to understand what they said is not one and
  needs no yes.
