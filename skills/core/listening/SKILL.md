# /listening

The domain of listening: taking in what another person says, in any setting,
whatever you plan to do afterward. Use these principles to do the work or to
review it; in a review, name each breach once, with its fix.

## Definitions

Listening has four purposes. The listening itself differs for each.

- **Appreciation:** listening for enjoyment, as with music.
- **Comprehension:** listening to understand information, so you can use it.
- **Empathy:** listening to understand how the speaker feels about their
  situation.
- **Evaluation:** listening while you judge what you hear, usually to give an
  answer or a solution.

## Principles

1. **Decide why you are listening this time, before you start.** The same
   words call for different listening depending on the purpose, so name it.
   Check again at suitable moments, since the purpose can change as the talk
   goes on.
2. **Do not mix empathy and evaluation.** They pull against each other, and
   the clash is a common source of conflict. Evaluation comes easily to
   people who are used to solving problems, so it can take over before you
   notice.
3. **When the purpose is empathy, hold back solutions.** A person who brings
   a hard moment may want to be understood first. Let them finish, even
   if it takes long, and do not rush to show that you can help.
4. **Show you followed by restating it in your own words.** Nodding is not
   enough. Say back their situation, and let them correct you. Done well,
   the speaker knows that you took in all of it.
5. **Offer advice only when they are ready for it, and ask first.** Once
   they have said what they came to say, they are more open, and advice can
   land. Ask whether they want your view, and only then give it.
