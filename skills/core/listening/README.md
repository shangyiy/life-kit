# listening

Principles for deciding why you listen, then listening, asking, and telling in
that order (`/listening`).

## Credit

- Leon (guest), "Influence Without Authority ｜ 人生元能力", 课代表立正
  (YouTube), 2021-07-27, https://youtu.be/CTcMvIZFQcw

These principles draw on the video:

- The four purposes of listening and their definitions, and keeping empathy
  and evaluation distinct (Gate, Definitions). The video attributes the four
  purposes to a Stanford study that is not cited here.
- Listen first (Principle 1): for empathy, hear the whole account and restate
  it; the three levels of empathy, ending in siding with the change the
  speaker wants; skipping this makes them feel you are not with them.
- Ask next (Principle 2): guide with questions from the speaker's position.
- Tell little (Principle 3): rushing in with solutions can look like proving
  you can help.
- The speaker's need decides where listening stops, and the purpose is
  checked again at suitable moments (Gate).

These are the author's own, not from the video:

- Asking whether they want help or your view before any guiding question or
  view. Siding with the change is the video's; that it needs no permission
  is the author's.
- Hearing the whole account whatever the purpose, not only for empathy, and
  restating only where the purpose calls for it.
- A request to judge counts as a yes: hear it all, then judge.
- Input with no speaker to ask needs no yes to be evaluated.
- The Guiding question definition, which exempts questions asked only to
  understand.
- Answering directly when the speaker plainly asks for an answer.
- Checking worth again alongside purpose, the noise filter and the harm
  override.
