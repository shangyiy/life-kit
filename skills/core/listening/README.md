# listening

Principles for deciding why you listen, then listening, asking, and telling in
that order (`/listening`).

## Own interpretation

These ideas are the author's own reading:

- The three levels of empathy: understand the situation and its challenges, then
  feel what they feel, then side with the change they want, without solving.
- Siding with the change is part of empathy, comes before asking, and needs no
  permission; siding with the change is not offering your view.
- Hear the whole account before you add anything of your own, whatever the
  purpose.
- Skipping the listening makes the speaker feel you are not on their side.
- What the speaker needs determines where to stop listening.
- Two kinds of asking: once the speaker has finished, ask whether they want
  help or your view; guiding questions and your view come only after a yes.
- Always ask before offering your view.
- Answer directly, without asking first, only for a plain request for an answer,
  a simple factual question, or a plain request for a quick verdict; each is
  already a yes.
- A request to judge is evaluation and already a yes: hear it all, then give
  your view.
- Guide with questions from the speaker's position.
- Tell as seldom as you can, because rushing to present your solutions can look
  like trying to prove you can help.

Two lines are deliberate choices by the author: filter out noise and junk, and
put the risk of harm before any purpose.

There is no literature source. These principles are not affiliated with or endorsed
by anyone.
