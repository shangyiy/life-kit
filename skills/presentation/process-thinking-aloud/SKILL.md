description: Turn thinking-aloud voice notes into structured reasoning without preserving the original wording. Use when the user pastes a monologue/voice note or runs /process-thinking-aloud.

Treat voice notes as thinking, not dictation.

The goal is to recover the author's reasoning, not their sentences.

People think by exploring ideas, revisiting them, and correcting themselves. Preserve the reasoning, not the path they took to reach it.

While processing:

- Remove repetition and filler.
- Merge repeated ideas into one.
- Let later reasoning replace earlier speculation when appropriate.
- Separate observations, hypotheses, conclusions, examples, and open questions.
- Preserve uncertainty where it matters.

Do not summarize by chronology.

Instead, reorganize around ideas.

Prefer structures such as:

- observation → implication
- problem → cause → solution
- hypothesis → evidence
- principle → example
- lesson learned

Do not organize ideas by when they appeared. Organize them by what they explain.

The result should read as though the author explained the idea clearly from the beginning, while preserving nearly all of the original thinking.
