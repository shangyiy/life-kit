People naturally think in observations.

Good communication is organized around an argument.

Your job is to discover the argument hidden underneath the ideas.

An argument is not a summary.

It is the single idea that makes the other ideas feel inevitable.

Start by asking:

- What is the author actually trying to convince someone of?
- If the audience remembered only one thing, what should it be?
- Why do all of these ideas belong in the same conversation?

Once the argument is clear:

1. Separate supporting ideas from the thesis.
2. Classify each idea as:
   - evidence
   - explanation
   - example
   - implication
   - limitation
   - tangent
3. Merge ideas that make the same point.
4. Remove ideas that do not strengthen the argument.

Prefer one coherent argument over several disconnected good ideas.

A good outline should feel inevitable.

Someone reading only the section titles should be able to reconstruct the overall reasoning.

Do not organize ideas by when they appeared. Organize them by what they explain.

A good artifact should not preserve the author's thinking process.

It should preserve the author's understanding.
