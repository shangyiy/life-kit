---
name: trend-catch-up
description: >-
  Catch a reader up on a fast-moving field by combining life-kit skills:
  gather what is new, keep the items that are good and can inspire the
  reader's work, and report each one's problem and what is new about it. Use
  for a catch-up report on a field, such as a weekly digest.
---
You catch one reader up on one field. You do not hold principles of your own;
each step applies a life-kit skill. The input is the field, the time window,
and the reader's context: their field, the problems they have, and work they
value. If the context is missing, ask for it, because steps 2 and 3 judge
every item against it.

1. **Gather.** Search the field's sources for items from the window, following
   `info-foraging`. Use scripts where a source allows it, such as a feed, an
   API or a saved query, because sharper queries and a list of good sources
   raise the rate of every later search (principle 4). Use attention signals
   such as links, votes, stars and who points to an item as scent to choose
   what to open, and open each item before judging it (principle 1). Leave a
   source once it yields less per unit of time than the average across
   sources (principle 2). Take the kinds of items that pay most for the time
   they take; add a kind only if it raises the overall rate (principle 3).
2. **Filter.** Keep an item only if it is a good idea by `idea-quality`: it is
   the core of the solution to a problem that keeps occurring (principle 1),
   it makes sense from first principles (principle 2), it actually solves the
   problem, even in part (principle 3), and it composes with other ideas
   (principle 4). Then keep it only if it can inspire this reader's work by
   `inspiring-idea`: it shares the relations of one of the reader's problems,
   not its surface (principle 2), and it is new to the reader (principle 6).
   How popular an item is does not decide either test (`inspiring-idea`).
   For each item dropped, note which point it misses (`inspiring-idea`, the
   Answer's "if no").
3. **Report.** Write by `readable`. Give each kept item its own point. Its
   first sentence says what problem the item solves and what is new about
   it: the usual approach's bottleneck and the one move that removes it, as
   in a good paper's statement of its contribution (`idea-quality` principle
   1, `inspiring-idea` Answer "Why"). The next sentences give the reader's
   problem it pairs with, which part matches which, the shared rule in the
   reader's own words, and where the pairing does not hold (`inspiring-idea`
   principles 3 and 4). Then give the first concrete step to try it in the
   reader's field (`inspiring-idea` Answer "How to apply it"). Link each item
   to its source. End with the sources searched and the items dropped, each
   with the point it missed, so the reader can check the filter.
